\begin{abstract}
Stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust spacetimes have been proposed as a
general-relativistic account of flat galactic rotation curves. We give a
theorem-level analysis of the constant-$\ell$ subclass, separating local
realizability from regularity, positivity, asymptotic completion, junction,
and observational requirements. The proposed nonzero constant ZAMO-speed
field solves neither the exact nor the retained-order equation on an open
region. Nevertheless, analytic flat equatorial traces exist locally away
from the axis. Their continuation is controlled by a sharp density
discriminant, a fold of the point-linearising map, axis regularity, and
quantitative span bounds. We prove automatic analyticity on every regular
sheet, exact Hadamard instability of elliptic Cauchy continuation, global
period obstructions for toroidal bodies, and Liouville rigidity of the
global regular-axis $\ell=0$ branch. The auxiliary equation possesses a
weighted variational principle, a De Donder--Weyl multisymplectic
formulation, Noether currents, and a Lagrangian boundary-data space; these
structures degenerate precisely at the density fold and must not be
identified with the physical Einstein--dust phase space. A zero-density
surface is only a candidate boundary: shell-free completion requires the
complete Darmois data and compatibility with a gauge-fixed exterior Ernst
problem. Finally, the local ZAMO speed does not determine a spectroscopic
rotation curve. The resulting hierarchy distinguishes proved identities,
global obstructions, conditional matching criteria, and questions reserved
for the numerical Part II.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{Intro}

The persistence of nearly flat orbital-speed profiles in the outer parts of many disk galaxies is one of the clearest manifestations of the galactic mass discrepancy~\cite{Lelli2016AJ}. The standard interpretation invokes a dominant non-luminous matter component~\cite{BertoneHooper2018RMP}, but it is nevertheless
legitimate to ask whether the global, nonlinear structure of general
relativity can modify the mass inferred from galactic kinematics. That question is more demanding than a comparison of a single velocity profile with data. A viable relativistic alternative must simultaneously solve the field equations, possess a physically acceptable matter source, remain regular on the rotation axis, admit a controlled outer
completion, and predict the frequency shifts actually measured by an
observer. We address this global viability problem for a specific subclass of stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust models.

The relevant solution space is the stationary, axisymmetric dust family
described by Winicour and in the standard exact-solution literature
\cite{Winicour1975JMP,Stephani2003Exact}. Balasin and Grumiller used its
rigidly rotating sector as a galactic model \cite{Balasin2008IJMPD}. A later
proposal by Astesiano, Cacciatori, Gorini, and Re enlarged the construction
to differential rotation by parametrizing the geometry with a field
$\eta(r,z)$ and a negative function $H(\eta)$
\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}. The speed measured by ZAMOs is $v=\eta/r$,
whereas the departure from rigid rotation is encoded in
$\ell(\eta)=H_{,\eta}/H$. In Sect.~4 of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, the
choice $\ell=\ellzero=a\vc/\RG$ is inserted into a low-velocity truncation of
the velocity-field equation (VFE). After a further near-edge replacement
$r\simeq\RG$, the resulting frozen-coefficient equation is reported to admit
the constant value $v=a\vc$, which is then associated with a flat rotation
curve. The same approximation is used to argue for a density reduction and
a finite density cutoff. These claims raise a precise question: can they be
realized by a solution of the parent constant-$\ell$ equations on a finite
region, and can that branch be completed to a regular isolated spacetime?

The proposal belongs to a broader strong-gravitomagnetic programme that
connects exact $(\eta,H)$ dust solutions, ZAMO kinematics, and weak-field
limits \cite{AstesianoRuggiero2022PRD,AstesianoRuggiero2022PRDL,
AstesianoRuggiero2025PRD}. Its claims must therefore be compared both with
the exact branch equations and with earlier global results for rotating
dust. Most decisively, Zingg, Aste, and Trautmann derived a finite-radius
density restriction for the general non-rigid Winicour family, argued that
an asymptotically flat dust region cannot extend to arbitrarily large
cylindrical radius under their hypotheses, and identified the tension caused
by prescribing both metric and normal data at a smooth vacuum interface
\cite{ZinggAsteTrautmann2007ASTP}. Caporali's global theorem, Pfister's
conditional analysis, and the distributional singularity results for rigid
van Stockum--Bonnor flow supply further precedents
\cite{Caporali1978PLA,Pfister2010CQG,BratekJalochaKutschera2007PRD}.
Accordingly, finite radial extent, asymptotic confinement, and matching
tension are not claimed here as new general mechanisms.

Several independent analyses have also identified serious obstacles for
large gravitomagnetic explanations of galactic dynamics. In physically
sourced weak-field disks, corrections of order $10^{-6}$ and the dangers of
incorrect scaling or homogeneous solutions were established in
Refs.~\cite{Ciotti2022ApJ,Lasenby2023CQG,GlampedakisJones2023CQG}; Rowland
gives earlier context for the low-velocity correspondence issue
\cite{Rowland2015IJMPD}. Costa, Nat\'ario, Frutos-Alfaro, and Soffel related
the large dragging of the rigid Balasin--Grumiller spacetime to its
asymptotic frame and axial singular structure \cite{CostaEtAl2023PRD}.
Broader stationary-spacetime arguments use both massive-particle dynamics
and lensing \cite{CostaNatario2024PRD,CostaNatario2026JPCS}. These analyses
do not directly test whether the specific constant-$\ell$ edge ansatz solves
its parent equations.

Complementary work has developed the nonlinear rotating-dust programme
itself. Ruggiero emphasized its lack of a direct Newtonian analogue and its
likely global singular structure \cite{Ruggiero2024JCAP}. Direct redshift
formulae and inverse-reconstruction difficulties precede the present
observability argument \cite{HerreraAguilarEtAl2013MNRAS,
Astesiano2022Observational}; a recent locally inertial and radially locked
construction gives a complementary local-observer programme
\cite{FontanaCacciatoriPeron2026arXiv}. Further studies have treated
observer-dependent velocities, lensing, explicit non-rigid subclasses,
topological regularity, and quasilocal pressure
\cite{ReGaloppo2026CQG,RuggieroAstesiano2025PRD,
GaloppoWiltshire2024arXiv,Galoppo2025EPJC,
GaloppoReWiltshire2025CQG}. Related approaches include nonstandard outer
boundary behaviour, dark-Coriolis profiles, and CMB multipoles
\cite{RePiattella2025Galaxies,BianchiRePiattella2026EPJP,
ReGaloppoDotti2025Galaxies}. Conically Minkowskian ends in the broader dust
family show that our asymptotic results concern the standard isolated
asymptotically flat class, not every possible outer geometry
\cite{FontanaScaliCacciatori2025PRD}.

The present contribution is narrower and complementary. We give the direct
exact and retained-order residual test of the proposed edge field; the
explicit sharp constant-$\ell$ density interval and quantitative annular
span bounds; the fold identification and its variational degeneration;
local analytic and semi-global Runge realization together with exact
Hadamard instability; a metric-only axis-jet obstruction; exact asymptotic
locking; toroidal period obstructions; and a gauge-fixed linearized Ernst
boundary test. These are branch-specific sharpenings and structural
results, not priority claims for the broad finite-radius or matching
mechanisms. The central question is whether the constant-$\ell$ subclass can
support a nonzero flat, or quantitatively approximately flat, ZAMO-speed
profile while maintaining the field equations, nonnegative density,
regularity, and an admissible exterior.

This question separates into eight parts. Does the proposed constant field
solve either VFE on an open region? Can transverse curvature support a flat
equatorial trace, and can that trace extend to a regular axis? What sharp
pointwise and radial-span restrictions follow from density positivity? What
regularity and stability properties govern continuation? Can the branch
possess a standard isolated asymptotically flat end? Does zero density yield
a shell-free boundary? Which topology and period conditions are required
for global Weyl and twist potentials? Finally, which auxiliary variational
structures survive the fold, and which local quantities determine an
observed spectroscopic shift?

The principal conclusions are collected in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-viability}. The proposed constant field fails both
equations on every open radial region, although flat traces exist locally
off the axis. Their continuation is constrained by the density fold, axis
regularity, and the invariant vertical-shape condition. Regular sheets are
automatically analytic but elliptic Cauchy continuation is Hadamard
unstable. The exact asymptotic end is locked to a single limiting value of
$rv$, while a finite density cutoff is only a candidate boundary. Toroidal
matter requires global period tests. The auxiliary action has exact
multisymplectic and Noether structure, but its Legendre map collapses at the
density fold and it is not the Einstein--dust phase space. The local ZAMO
speed does not by itself determine a spectroscopic rotation curve.

Sections~\ref{sec:constant-field-obstruction}--\ref{sec:approx-flat-obstruction}

\section{Rotating-dust framework and the constant-\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} reduction}\label{sec:model-framework}

This section identifies the precise field-equation framework underlying the subsequent viability analysis. We first recall the exact velocity-field equation for the general $(\eta,H)$ family and then, specialize it to the constant-$\ell$ branch. We also distinguish the exact reduced equation from the retained-order equation used in Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, thereby separating consequences of the constant-$\ell$ restriction from those of the additional low-velocity approximation. We begin by fixing the notation and conventions used throughout. We work with the exact stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust family described in Refs.~\cite{Winicour1975JMP,Stephani2003Exact,Astesiano2022EPJC}. We use
coordinates $(t,\phi,r,z)$ adapted to the stationary and axial Killing
fields $k=\partial_t$ and $m=\partial_\phi$. In these coordinates, the
circular orbits of $m$ have period $2\pi$. We use signature $(-,+,+,+)$
and units $c=1$, while retaining $G_{\rm N}$ in gravitational actions and
junction formulae. The coordinates $t,r,z$ and the potential $\eta$ have
dimensions of length; $\phi$, $v$, $H$, and $\mu$ are dimensionless; and
$\Omega$ and $\ell$ have dimensions of inverse length. In particular,
$x=r\ell$ is dimensionless. The
circular line element takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-block-metric}
ds^2=g_{tt}(r,z)\,dt^2+2g_{t\phi}(r,z)\,dt\,d\phi+g_{\phi\phi}(r,z)\,d\phi^2 +e^{\mu(r,z)}\bigl(dr^2+dz^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
The metric functions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-block-metric} are not
independent. The matter source is pressureless dust,
$T_{\mu\nu}=\rho\,u_\mu u_\nu$, where $\rho$ is the dust density and $u$
is the dust four-velocity. Since the dust worldlines are circular, their
four-velocity is tangent to a linear combination of the two Killing
directions. The exact solution family can be parametrized by a scalar
field $\eta(r,z)$ and a function $H(\eta)$, with
\begin{equation}
H=g(k+\Omega m,k+\Omega m)<0,\qquad
\eta=g(k+\Omega m,m),
\label{eq:model-H-eta-definitions}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega(\eta)$ is the coordinate angular velocity of the dust. Thus, $H$ is the squared norm of the unnormalized dust-flow direction
$k+\Omega m$, while $\eta$ is its scalar product with the axial Killing
field. The inequality $H<0$ expresses the timelike character of the dust flow. The normalized four-velocity and the relation between $\Omega$ and $H$ are therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-velocity-relations}
u=\frac{k+\Omega m}{\sqrt{-H}},\qquad
\frac{d\Omega}{d\eta}=\frac{H_{,\eta}}{2\eta}.
\end{equation}
Once $\eta(r,z)$, $H(\eta)$, and the corresponding angular velocity $\Omega(\eta)$ are specified, the metric coefficients in the $(t\phi)$-sector are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-metric-components}
g_{\phi\phi}=\frac{r^2-\eta^2}{-H},\quad
g_{t\phi}=\eta-\Omega g_{\phi\phi},\quad
g_{tt}=H-2\Omega\eta+\Omega^2 g_{\phi\phi}.
\end{equation}
The remaining metric function $\mu$ is determined, up to an additive
constant, by the remaining Einstein equations. For later use, let $G=(g_{AB})_{A,B\in\{t,\phi\}}$ denote the metric block associated with the two Killing directions. The parametrization above implies the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-metric-identities}
\det G=g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}-g_{t\phi}^2=-r^2.
\end{equation}
Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} introduces the ZAMO-measured speed $v$ and the logarithmic derivative $\ell$ through
\begin{equation}\label{v_ell}
v(r,z)=\frac{\eta(r,z)}{r},\quad
\ell(\eta)=\frac{H_{,\eta}}{H}.
\end{equation}
In this notation, the exact velocity-field equation (VFE) (see Eq.~(3.1) of
Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}) can be written as
\begin{align}
\mathcal{V}_{\ell}[v]:={}&\ell'(\eta)\left(\frac{1}{v}-v\right)
\left[r^{2}v_{z}^{2}+(rv_{r}+v)^{2}\right]
+\ell(\eta)\Bigg[\left(\frac{1}{v}-v\right)
\left(r v_{zz}+r v_{rr}+3v_{r}\right)
-\left(\frac{1}{v}+v\right)\frac{r}{v}
\left(v_{z}^{2}+v_{r}^{2}\right)
+\frac{2}{r}\Bigg]
\nonumber\\
&+2\left(v_{zz}+v_{rr}+\frac{v_{r}}{r}-\frac{v}{r^{2}}\right)
\label{eq:full-vfe}
\end{align}
with the field equation $\mathcal{V}_{\ell}[v]=0$. Throughout the local analysis, we assume $r>0$, $v\in C^2$, and $0<v<1$. The strict inequality avoids the singular value $v=0$ in expressions containing $v^{-1}$ and implements the subluminal interpretation. We now impose $\ell(\eta)\equiv\ellzero$ with $\ellzero\in\mathbb R$. Using Eq.~\eqref{v_ell}, the definition of $\ell$ integrates to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-l-H}
H(\eta)=-C e^{\ellzero\eta},\qquad C>0.
\end{equation}
For $\ellzero\neq 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-velocity-relations} further gives,
on every interval not crossing $\eta=0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-l-Omega}
\Omega(\eta)=\Omega_*-\frac{C\ellzero}{2}\operatorname{Ei}(\ellzero\eta),
\end{equation}
whereas $\Omega$ is constant when $\ellzero=0$. Here, $\operatorname{Ei}$ denotes the exponential integral. Since $\ell'=0$, the exact constant-$\ell$ VFE is $\EE[v]=0$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-constant-l}
\EE[v]:=\left[2+\ellzero r\left(\frac{1}{v}-v\right)\right]\Drz v
+\left[\frac{2}{r}+3\ellzero\left(\frac{1}{v}-v\right)\right]v_{r}
-\ellzero r\left(\frac{1}{v^{2}}+1\right)\lvert\gradRZ v\rvert^{2}
+\frac{2\ellzero}{r}-\frac{2v}{r^{2}},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\Drz v:=v_{rr}+v_{zz},
\qquad
\lvert\gradRZ v\rvert^{2}:=v_{r}^{2}+v_{z}^{2}.
\end{equation}
If we discard the terms that are higher order in the low-velocity expansion
used in Sect.~4 of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, we obtain the retained-order equation $\ET[v]=0$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncated-operator}
\ET[v]:=\left(2+\frac{\ellzero r}{v}\right)\Drz v
+\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3\ellzero}{v}\right)v_{r}
-\frac{\ellzero r}{v^{2}}\lvert\gradRZ v\rvert^{2}
+\frac{2\ellzero}{r}-\frac{2v}{r^{2}}.
\end{equation}
In Sect.~4 of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, the constant value
\begin{equation}
\ell=\ellzero=\frac{a\vc}{\RG}
\end{equation}
is adopted, where $\vc$ is a characteristic stellar orbital speed,
$\RG$ is a radial scale comparable to the galactic radius, and $a$ is
a dimensionless parameter. With this parameter choice, the retained-order equation $\ET[v]=0$ coincides with Eq.~(4.3) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}. The difference between the
exact and retained-order operators is the algebraic identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remainder-identity}
\EE[v]-\ET[v]=-\ellzero\left[rv\Drz v+3vv_{r}+r\lvert\gradRZ v\rvert^{2}\right].
\end{equation}
In particular, the discarded part vanishes for every spatially constant
field. All terms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exact-constant-l} and \eqref{eq:truncated-operator} have dimension $L^{-2}$, since $[\ellzero]=L^{-1}$ and $v$ is dimensionless.

\section{Principal constraints on the constant-\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} branch}\label{sec:principal-result}

The branch is governed by several logically independent tests: local field
equations, inversion of the auxiliary map, density, axis jets, continuation,
outer geometry, boundary data, and observability. The following synthesis
keeps those levels separate. Every item inherits the hypotheses of the cited
result; in particular, no local construction is being promoted to a global
galaxy and no numerical evidence is used as a proof.

\begin{theorem}[Synthesis theorem and structural dichotomies]
\label{thm:main-viability}
Consider the exact stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust family of
Sect.~\ref{sec:model-framework}, with positive subluminal ZAMO speed and
constant $\ell=\ellzero$. The following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A nonzero spatially constant field cannot satisfy either the exact or
retained-order constant-$\ell$ VFE on an open set with nontrivial radial
projection. The proposed edge calibration makes its residual vanish only
on $r=\RG$. On the regular jet stratum $A\neq0$, by contrast, the exact VFE
is involutive and point-equivalent to a linear elliptic equation; the fold
$A=0$ is a genuine symbol-rank jump.

\item At every regular noncritical point, $\rho\geqslant0$ is equivalent to
the sharp interval in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain}. The lower endpoint is
simultaneously a nondegenerate Whitney fold of the linearising map, whereas
strict positive density selects its orientation-preserving inverse sheet.

\item Every analytic exactly flat equatorial trace satisfying the strict
off-axis inversion conditions has a local analytic realization. Conversely,
every $C^2$ solution on a connected invertible sheet is automatically
analytic, and an open constant-$\eta$ plateau propagates throughout that
sheet. Continuation from unrestricted smooth Cauchy data is nevertheless
Hadamard unstable. Elliptic Runge approximation gives semi-global
realizations only on a protected compact core with quantitative physical
margins; it gives no control on the surrounding region.

\item A $C^2$ metric with a regular effective axial action forces
$H'(0)=0$ on every branch reaching the axis. Hence a nontrivial constant
logarithmic derivative can reach a regular axis only when $\ellzero=0$.
No axis-connected branch can carry a nonzero exactly flat trace. Under the
strong complete-half-plane, regular-axis, and decay assumptions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ellzero-liouville}, even the global $\ellzero=0$ branch is
Minkowski vacuum.

\item Under reflection symmetry, positive density, subluminality, and the
stated vertical-maximum and logarithmic-slope hypotheses, approximately
flat traces have finite radial span. The nonpositive-$\ellzero$ bound is
sharp on its closure and strict for each $\ellzero<0$. For
$\ellzero>0$ the old comparison bound has a quantified strict gap, while
the exactly flat supremum is $2/(1+v_0^2)$ and is approached by genuine
analytic solutions. The exact positive-$\ellzero$, positive-tolerance
control optimum remains open.

\item If a nonzero constant-$\ellzero$ sheet itself reaches a standard
asymptotically flat end and remains uniformly subluminal, then
$\eta=rv$ locks to $q=-\ellzero^{-1}\log C$ and $v=O(r^{-1})$; the
retained-order logarithmic branch cannot supply such an end. If density is
nonnegative in outer neighbourhoods, the end is eventually vacuum and
locally flat. Its possible nonzero $q$ is an affine helical holonomy, not a
constructed string source, and it cannot extend within the same flat region
to a regular axial fixed point.

\item A compact connected body whose meridional image is a disk separated
from the axis is a solid torus. Its vacuum orbit surface is not simply
connected, and global Weyl and twist potentials require explicit period
tests. At any finite boundary, $\rho=0$ is only a candidate condition:
shell-free gluing is equivalent to equality of the full first and second
fundamental forms. The linearized exterior Ernst data must additionally lie
in a Calder\'on Lagrangian graph. None of these necessary conditions
constructs a nonlinear isolated exterior.

\item The linear auxiliary equation possesses an exact weighted action,
Noether currents, covariant Hamiltonian variables, and a multisymplectic
current formula, but its Legendre map collapses at the density fold and it
is not the Einstein--dust phase space. The physical theory is instead the
Einstein--Hilbert--GHY action coupled to vortical Brown dust; equality of its
boundary momenta is equivalent to the complete Darmois condition. In the
different EMDA theory, neutral axidilaton rigidity holds only under the
global hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-neutral-axidilaton-rigidity};
duality transformations do not by themselves produce a rotating-dust
completion.

\item The local scalar $v$ does not determine an emitter--observer
frequency shift independently of the metric, the two observer frames, and
the connecting null ray. A spectroscopic rotation curve therefore requires
the completed spacetime and a specified forward problem.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the constant-field, constant-$\ell$ edge construction does
not by itself define a globally viable isolated rotating-dust galaxy. This
conclusion does not exclude nonconstant-$\ell$ solutions, finite interiors
with separately constructed exteriors, pressure-supported or kinetic
matter, nonstandard asymptotics, or a spectroscopic curve obtained from a
complete photon-propagation model.
\end{theorem}

\section{Failure of the constant-field edge ansatz}
\label{sec:constant-field-obstruction}

This section examines whether a nonzero spatially constant ZAMO speed can
satisfy the constant-$\ell$ velocity-field equation on a finite region.
We first establish a pointwise obstruction on any open radial interval and then apply it to the specific edge calibration and frozen-coefficient
approximation of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}. Finally, we determine the
scope of the obstruction by allowing $\ell$ to vary, showing that the
failure of the constant-field ansatz is a consequence of the
constant-$\ell$ restriction rather than of the general $(\eta,H)$ family.


\subsection{Pointwise obstruction on an open region}

We begin by showing that a nonzero spatially constant field cannot satisfy the constant-$\ell$ VFE on any open radial interval.

\begin{proposition}[Constant-field obstruction at fixed $\ell$]
\label{prop:constant-l-obstruction}
Let $I\subset(0,\infty)$ and $J\subset\mathbb{R}$ be nonempty open
intervals, and let $\ellzero\in\mathbb{R}$ be constant. There is no
constant $v_{0}\neq 0$ for which the field
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-field}
v(r,z)\equiv v_{0}\qquad \text{on } I\times J
\end{equation}
satisfies the retained-order VFE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truncated-vfe-zero}
\ET[v]=0\qquad \text{pointwise on } I\times J.
\end{equation}
The same conclusion holds for the untruncated constant-$\ell$ VFE
$\EE[v]=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the field in Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-field}, all first and second
spatial derivatives vanish, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivatives-zero}
v_{r}=v_{z}=v_{rr}=v_{zz}=0.
\end{equation}
Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:truncated-operator} leads to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-residual}
\ET[v_{0}]=\frac{2}{r^{2}}\left(\ellzero r-v_{0}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:truncated-vfe-zero} would require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:impossible-identity}
\ellzero r=v_{0}\qquad \text{for every } r\in I.
\end{equation}
Because $I$ is an open interval, it contains two distinct radii
$r_{1}\neq r_{2}$. Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:impossible-identity} at these radii and subtracting gives $\ellzero(r_{1}-r_{2})=0$, so $\ellzero=0$. Substitution back into Eq.~\eqref{eq:impossible-identity} gives $v_{0}=0$, contradicting the hypothesis $v_{0}\neq 0$. Hence, no nonzero spatially constant field can solve the retained-order VFE on $I\times J$. Finally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:remainder-identity} and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:derivatives-zero} imply $\EE[v_{0}]=\ET[v_{0}]$. Therefore, the same contradiction applies to the untruncated
constant-$\ell$ VFE. This also shows that no term omitted in the
low-velocity truncation can repair the constant-field ansatz.
\end{proof}

The proposition excludes a nonzero constant field on any open region.
It is also useful to identify more precisely the set on which the
corresponding residual may vanish.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:zero-set}
Fix $v_{0}\neq 0$. The residual of a constant field is Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-residual}. Consequently, the following statements hold
\begin{enumerate}
\item
if $\ellzero=0$, the residual is nonzero for every $r>0$;
\item
if $\ellzero\neq 0$ but $v_{0}/\ellzero\leqslant 0$, the residual is nonzero for every physical radius $r>0$;
\item
if $\ellzero\neq 0$ and $v_{0}/\ellzero>0$, the residual vanishes only on the single cylinder
\begin{equation}
r=r_{*}:=\frac{v_{0}}{\ellzero}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
In every case, the zero set has empty interior in the $(r,z)$ domain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:constant-residual} vanishes if and only if
$\ellzero r-v_{0}=0$. The three cases follow immediately from $r>0$.
A set of the form $\{r=r_{*}\}$ is codimension one and therefore contains no open subset of the $(r,z)$ plane.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The proposed edge calibration and frozen coefficients}

The parameter choice in Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-choice}
\ellzero=\frac{a\vc}{\RG},\qquad
v_{0}=a\vc=\ellzero\RG.
\end{equation}
Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-choice} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-residual} gives the exact residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-residual}
\ET[a\vc]=\EE[a\vc]=\frac{2a\vc}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{r}{\RG}-1\right).
\end{equation}
For $a\vc\neq 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-residual} vanishes if and only if $r=\RG$. Thus, the value $v=a\vc$ is compatible with the constant-$\ell$ VFE at the single edge cylinder $r=\RG$, but not on any annulus containing more than one radius. To display precisely what is lost in the near-edge replacement, we write
\begin{equation}
r=\RG(1+\delta),\qquad \delta:=\frac{r-\RG}{\RG}.
\end{equation}
Then, Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-residual} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-residual}
\ET[a\vc]=\frac{2a\vc}{\RG^{2}}\left(\delta-2\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{3})\right).
\end{equation}
The near-edge Eq.~(4.5) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} is obtained by
freezing the dimensionless factors at $r/\RG=1$ and replacing
$1/\RG$ by $1/r$ at that order. This operation sets the linear mismatch
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta-residual} to zero. It therefore produces a
frozen-coefficient equation for which $v=a\vc$ is a constant solution.
Equation~\eqref{eq:delta-residual} shows that this is a pointwise or
zeroth-order edge relation, not a solution of the parent retained-order
equation on a finite radial interval. Nor is it consistent to promote the near-edge replacement $\RG\simeq r$ to an identity throughout an interval while continuing to use Eq.~\eqref{eq:truncated-operator}. Such a promotion would change $\ellzero=a\vc/\RG$ into a radially varying quantity $a\vc/r$, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:truncated-operator} was derived after imposing $\ell'(\eta)=0$. Once $\ell$ varies, the first term of the full VFE in Eq.~\eqref{eq:full-vfe} must be restored. A constant value may still serve as the leading term of a local expansion in a shrinking neighbourhood of $r=\RG$, for example, when
$\delta=O(\varepsilon)$ and a nonconstant correction is included at the
next order. What Proposition~\ref{prop:constant-l-obstruction} excludes is the stronger statement that an exactly constant field solves the constant-$\ell$ VFE throughout an open outer region. It does not, however, rule out a configuration that is nonconstant in the full $(r,z)$ domain but has an exactly flat equatorial trace, since transverse curvature may enter the exact VFE through the vertical derivatives. Section~\ref{sec:exact-flat-traces} addresses this remaining possibility without using the frozen-coefficient approximation.

\subsection{Role of the constant-\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} assumption}

The constant-$\ell$ assumption is essential to Proposition~\ref{prop:constant-l-obstruction}. To isolate the role of the constant-$\ell$ assumption, we return to the full VFE in Eq.~\eqref{eq:full-vfe} and impose a spatially constant field $v\equiv
v_0$ with $0<v_0<1$, while allowing $\ell=\ell(\eta)$ to vary. Since $\eta=rv_0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:full-vfe} reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l-ode-r}
v_{0}(1-v_{0}^{2})\ell'(\eta)+\frac{2\ell(\eta)}{r}-\frac{2v_{0}}{r^{2}}=0.
\end{equation}
Using $r=\eta/v_{0}$ and dividing by $v_{0}\neq 0$ gives the linear ODE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l-ode-eta}
(1-v_{0}^{2})\ell'(\eta)+\frac{2}{\eta}\ell(\eta)=\frac{2v_{0}^{2}}{\eta^{2}}.
\end{equation}
After division by $1-v_{0}^{2}$, an integrating factor is
\begin{equation}
\mu(\eta)=\exp\left(\int\frac{2}{(1-v_{0}^{2})\eta}\,d\eta\right)
=\eta^{\frac{2}{1-v_{0}^{2}}},\qquad \eta>0.
\end{equation}
Multiplying Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-ode-eta} by this factor yields
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{d\eta}\left(\eta^{\frac{2}{1-v_{0}^{2}}}\ell(\eta)\right)
=\frac{2v_{0}^{2}}{1-v_{0}^{2}}\eta^{\frac{2}{1-v_{0}^{2}}-2}.
\end{equation}
Integration gives the complete local family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l-supporting-constant-v}
\ell(\eta)=\frac{2v_{0}^{2}}{1+v_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{\eta}+C\eta^{-\frac{2}{1-v_{0}^{2}}},
\end{equation}
where $C$ is an integration constant with the dimensions needed to make
$\ell$ an inverse length. Conversely, substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-ode-eta} verifies that every member of Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-supporting-constant-v} supports the constant field at the level of the VFE. Since $\ell=H_{,\eta}/H$, each admissible $\ell(\eta)$ determines, at least locally, a corresponding negative function $H(\eta)$. For any $H_{*}>0$ one may set $H(\eta)=-H_{*}\exp\!\left(\int^{\eta}\ell(s)\,ds\right)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:l-supporting-constant-v} therefore
shows that an exactly constant velocity field is not forbidden by the VFE in general, but is incompatible with a constant $\ell$ on an open radial interval. The functions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:l-supporting-constant-v} are generally singular as $\eta\to 0$, so their global physical viability remains a separate question.


\section{Density-positivity bounds and radial cutoffs}
\label{sec:density-positivity}

We next examine the restrictions imposed by the requirement of nonnegative dust density. The exact density formula allows its sign to be isolated in a simple algebraic factor, from which we derive a sharp admissible interval for the product $r\ell$. For constant-$\ell$ configurations with an exactly flat equatorial trace, this interval in turn yields finite radial cutoffs and permits a direct comparison with the parameter choice of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}. Let us first recall that the exact density formula of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-density}
8\pi G_N\rho=\frac{1}{4g_{rr}}\left[\frac{\eta^{2}}{r^{2}}(2-\eta\ell)^{2}-r^{2}\ell^{2}\right]\frac{\eta_{r}^{2}+\eta_{z}^{2}}{\eta^{2}}.
\end{equation}
Here, $G_N$ denotes Newton's gravitational constant. Since
$g_{rr}=e^{\mu}$, the metric coefficient $g_{rr}$ is finite and strictly
positive at every regular point. Consequently, it affects the magnitude
of the density but not its sign. To isolate the remaining factors that
control the sign of $\rho$, consider a regular point with $r>0$ and
introduce $\eta=rv$, and $x:=r\ell$. For $0<v<1$, one has $\eta\neq 0$. Substitution into the square bracket in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-density} gives the dimensionless quadratic
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-polynomial}
\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)=4v^{2}-4v^{3}x-(1-v^{4})x^{2}.
\end{equation}
Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-density} can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-sign-decomposition}
\rho=\frac{\eta_{r}^{2}+\eta_{z}^{2}}{32\pi G_N\,g_{rr}\eta^{2}}
\mathcal{N}_{v}(x).
\end{equation}
The prefactor multiplying $\mathcal{N}_{v}$ is nonnegative, and it is
strictly positive precisely when $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq0$.

\begin{proposition}[Density-positivity domain]
\label{prop:density-positivity-domain}
Let $p$ be a regular point with $r>0$, $0<v<1$, and finite
$\ell(\eta)$. Set $x=r\ell$ at $p$. Then, the algebraic density factor
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-polynomial} is nonnegative if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-domain}
-\frac{2v}{1-v^{2}}\leqslant r\ell\leqslant\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$ at $p$, then
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} is equivalent to $\rho\geqslant 0$ at that point. Under the same nondegeneracy hypothesis, $\rho>0$ at $p$ if and only if both inequalities in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} are strict. If either inequality is saturated, then $\rho=0$ at that point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
To determine the sign of $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-polynomial}, we factor it as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-factorization}
\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)=\bigl[2v-(1+v^{2})x\bigr]
\bigl[2v+(1-v^{2})x\bigr].
\end{equation}
Because $0<v<1$, both terms $1-v^{2}$ and $1+v^{2}$ are strictly positive. Moreover, the two factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-factorization} vanish at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-roots}
x_{-}:=-\frac{2v}{1-v^{2}},\qquad
x_{+}:=\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}},
\end{equation}
respectively, and $x_{-}<0<x_{+}$. Equivalently, Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-polynomial} is a quadratic polynomial in $x$ with leading coefficient $-(1-v^{4})<0$ for $0<v<1$. Since $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)$ is therefore strictly concave and has two distinct real roots $x_{-}<x_{+}$, it is nonnegative precisely for $x\in[x_{-},x_+]$ and negative outside this interval. Hence, $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)\geqslant 0$ if and only if $x_{-}\leqslant x\leqslant x_{+}$, which, after substituting $x=r\ell$, gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain}. Moreover, $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)>0$ for $x_{-}<x<x_{+}$, while $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)=0$ at the endpoints. At a regular noncritical point, where $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$, the term $(\eta_{r}^{2}+\eta_{z}^{2})/(32\pi G_N\,g_{rr}\eta^{2})$ is strictly positive. Indeed, $G_N>0$, $g_{rr}>0$, and $\eta^{2}>0$, while the numerator
is positive by the noncriticality assumption. It follows that $\rho$ and
$\mathcal{N}_{v}$ have the same sign, which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

At a critical point of $\eta$, where $\nabla_{rz}\eta=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-sign-decomposition} gives $\rho=0$ independently of the sign of
$\mathcal{N}_{v}(r\ell)$. Therefore, Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} is not a necessary condition for $\rho\geqslant 0$ at such points. It is, however, necessary and sufficient for the nonnegativity of the algebraic density factor, and, at every regular noncritical point, it is equivalent to $\rho\geqslant 0$. For strictly positive density no qualification is needed, since $\rho>0$ itself implies $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$.

Under continuity and nonnegative density, a violation of the bound at a critical point necessarily extends to a local vacuum region.

\begin{corollary}[Failure of the bound forces a local vacuum plateau]
\label{cor:vacuum-plateau}
Let $U$ be an open set of regular points on which $0<v<1$ and
$\rho\geqslant 0$, and assume that the functions entering
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-sign-decomposition} are continuous on $U$.
If Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} fails at a point $p\in U$, then there
exists a connected open neighbourhood $V\subset U$ of $p$ on which $\nabla_{rz}\eta=0$, and $\rho=0$. In particular, if $\eta$ is not locally constant on any nonempty open subset of $U$, then Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} must hold throughout $U$, including at critical points.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain}, failure of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} at $p$ implies
$\mathcal{N}_{v}(r\ell)<0$ there. By continuity, there exists a connected
open neighbourhood $V\subset U$ of $p$ on which
$\mathcal{N}_{v}(r\ell)<0$. Since $G_N>0$, $g_{rr}>0$, and
$\eta^2>0$ on $V$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-sign-decomposition} would give
$\rho<0$ at any point where $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$, contradicting the
assumption $\rho\geqslant 0$. Hence, $\nabla_{rz}\eta=0$ throughout $V$, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-sign-decomposition} then gives $\rho=0$ on $V$. Because $V$ is connected, the vanishing of $\nabla_{rz}\eta$ implies that $\eta$ is constant on $V$. Therefore, if $\eta$ is not locally constant on any nonempty open subset of $U$, the bound Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} cannot fail at any point of $U$.
\end{proof}

Having clarified the role of critical points, it is useful to rewrite the
positivity bound in terms of the combination $\eta\ell$. Since
$\eta=rv$ and $v>0$, one has $\eta\ell=v(r\ell)$. Multiplying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} by $v$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-l-domain}
-\frac{2v^{2}}{1-v^{2}}\leqslant \eta\ell\leqslant\frac{2v^{2}}{1+v^{2}}.
\end{equation}
This form makes the low-velocity behaviour of the positivity bound
transparent. As $v\to 0^{+}$, the allowed interval for $\eta\ell$ has width of order $v^{2}$, with endpoints $-2v^{2}-2v^{4}+O(v^{6})\leqslant \eta\ell
\leqslant 2v^{2}-2v^{4}+O(v^{6})$, or equivalently $-2v-2v^{3}+O(v^{5})\leqslant r\ell
\leqslant 2v-2v^{3}+O(v^{5})$. Thus, the asymmetry between the lower and upper bounds for $r\ell$ first appears at cubic order in $v$. The preceding expressions use the positive-speed convention $0<v<1$ adopted throughout this analysis. If $v$ is instead regarded as an oriented quantity with $-1<v<0$, the same two algebraic roots are obtained, but their order is reversed. In that convention, the positivity domain can be written without reference to their ordering as
\begin{equation}
\min\!\left\{-\frac{2v}{1-v^{2}},
\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}\right\}\leqslant r\ell\leqslant
\max\!\left\{-\frac{2v}{1-v^{2}},\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
In what follows, we retain the positive-speed convention $0<v<1$. We now specialize the density-positivity bound to an exactly flat equatorial trace with constant $\ell$. In this setting, the bound translates directly into a finite radial range whenever $\ell\neq 0$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite positivity radius for an exactly flat equatorial trace]
\label{cor:flat-trace-density-radius}
Let $I\subset(0,\infty)$ be an interval on which $v(r,0)\equiv v_{0}$ with $0<v_{0}<1$, and let $\ell\equiv\ellzero$ be constant. No assumption on $v_z$ or $v_{zz}$ is required. Nonnegative density along the trace is then equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)]
if $\ellzero>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-l-cutoff}
0<r\leqslant R_{\rho}^{(+)}:=\frac{2v_{0}}
{\ellzero(1+v_{0}^{2})};
\end{equation}
\item[(ii)]
If $\ellzero<0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:negative-l-cutoff}
0<r\leqslant R_{\rho}^{(-)}:=\frac{2v_{0}}
{|\ellzero|(1-v_{0}^{2})};
\end{equation}
\item[(iii)]
If $\ellzero=0$, the algebraic density factor is
$\mathcal{N}_{v_{0}}(0)=4v_{0}^{2}>0$, and remains positive throughout the trace.
\end{enumerate}
In cases (i) and (ii), the density vanishes at the corresponding cutoff
radius and is negative at every regular trace point beyond it.
\end{corollary}

This is a pointwise sign result for an exactly flat equatorial trace. It
does not by itself establish that the trace extends to a global solution
or that the zero-density radius defines a matchable material boundary.

\begin{proof}
Since $\eta(r,0)=rv_{0}$, differentiation along the trace gives $\eta_r(r,0)=v_{0}$. Therefore, $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$ there, irrespective of the transverse derivative $\eta_z$. Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain} applies without the critical-point exception. For $\ellzero>0$, the condition $r\ellzero>0$ ensures that the lower bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} is always satisfied, whereas the upper bound reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:positive-l-cutoff}. For $\ellzero<0$, one has $r\ellzero<0$, so the upper bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain} is always satisfied, whereas the lower bound becomes
\begin{equation}
r|\ellzero|\leqslant\frac{2v_{0}}{1-v_{0}^{2}},
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to Eq.~\eqref{eq:negative-l-cutoff}. For
$\ellzero=0$, direct substitution gives $\mathcal{N}_{v_{0}}(0)=4v_{0}^{2}>0$. Finally, the endpoint and exterior sign statements follow from the
strict concavity and root classification in
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain}.
\end{proof}

We next specialize the finite-radius bound to the parameter choice adopted
in Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, which allows the exact cutoff to be
compared directly with the retained-order prediction.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:paper-density-cutoff}
Let us assume $a>0$, $\vc>0$, $0<a\vc<1$. Furthermore, we set
$\ellzero=a\vc/\RG$, and suppose that, on a radial interval $I\subset(0,\infty)$, the equatorial trace satisfies $v(r,0)\equiv v_{0}=a\vc$. Then, the exact algebraic density factor is nonnegative at precisely those
radii $r\in I$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-exact-density-cutoff}
0<r\leqslant R_{\rho}^{\mathrm{exact}}:=\frac{2\RG}{1+a^{2}\vc^{2}}.
\end{equation}
If $R_{\rho}^{\mathrm{exact}}\in I$, the density vanishes at the cutoff
radius, while at every regular point of the trace with $r>R_{\rho}^{\mathrm{exact}}$ it is negative. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-cutoff-expansion}
R_{\rho}^{\mathrm{exact}}=2\RG\left[
1-a^{2}\vc^{2}+O\!\left(a^{4}\vc^{4}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Hence, $2\RG$ is the leading low-velocity approximation to the exact
density-positivity cutoff.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\ellzero>0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:positive-l-cutoff} applies. Substituting
$v_{0}=a\vc$ and $\ellzero=a\vc/\RG$ into the expression for $R_{\rho}^{(+)}$ gives
\begin{equation}
R_{\rho}^{(+)}=\frac{2\RG}{1+a^{2}\vc^{2}},
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-exact-density-cutoff}. The sign statements
follow from Corollary~\ref{cor:flat-trace-density-radius}. Finally, the
geometric-series expansion
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{1+a^{2}\vc^{2}}=1-a^{2}\vc^{2}+O(a^{4}\vc^{4})
\end{equation}
gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-cutoff-expansion}.
\end{proof}

The positivity bound should not be interpreted as implying that the algebraic density factor necessarily decreases when $\ell$ departs from zero. At fixed $v$, the case $\ell=0$ corresponds to $x=r\ell=0$, for which $\mathcal{N}_{v}(0)=4v^{2}$. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-polynomial},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-difference}
\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)-\mathcal{N}_{v}(0)=-4v^{3}x-(1-v^{4})x^{2}.
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{N}_{v}$ is a concave quadratic in $x$, its maximum occurs at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-numerator-maximum}
x_{*}=-\frac{2v^{3}}{1-v^{4}},\qquad
\mathcal{N}_{v}(x_{*})=\frac{4v^{2}}{1-v^{4}}>\mathcal{N}_{v}(0).
\end{equation}
More precisely, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-difference} shows that $\mathcal{N}_{v}(x)$ exceeds its value at $\ell=0$ precisely for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greater-than-rigid-interval}
-\frac{4v^{3}}{1-v^{4}}<x<0.
\end{equation}
This interval lies strictly inside the positivity domain of Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-domain}. Hence, a sufficiently small negative
value of $r\ell$ increases the algebraic density factor relative to its
value at $\ell=0$. This comparison concerns only the algebraic factor
$\mathcal{N}_{v}$. The full density also depends on $g_{rr}$, which
generally changes when $H$ is varied. Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain} should therefore be understood as a sign criterion, rather than as a
monotonicity statement for the density as a function of $\ell$.

\section{Exactly flat equatorial traces: local existence and global obstructions}
\label{sec:exact-flat-traces}

Proposition~\ref{prop:constant-l-obstruction} does not exclude a
nonconstant solution whose equatorial trace is exactly flat. We first
show that such traces can be realized locally on patches separated from
the rotation axis. We then examine whether they can be extended beyond
this local setting. Regularity at the axis excludes every nontrivial
branch with $\ellzero\neq 0$, while an analytic-continuation argument
rules out an axis-connected exactly flat trace for the remaining case
$\ellzero=0$. Finally, for branches confined to annular regions, we
combine density positivity with vertical localization to derive a finite
bound on the radial extent of an exactly flat trace.

\subsection{Local analytic realization of an exactly flat trace}
For constant $\ell=\ellzero$, it is useful to recast Eq.~(2.10) of
Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} in terms of an auxiliary potential
$\mathcal F(r,z)$. On any patch where $\eta>0$, the relation between
$\mathcal F$ and $\eta$ can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-l-Phi}
\mathcal F(r,z)=\Phi_{\ellzero}(r,\eta(r,z)),\qquad
\Phi_{\ellzero}(r,\eta):=2\eta+\ellzero r^{2}\log\!\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{*}}\right)-\frac{\ellzero}{2}\eta^{2},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{*}>0$ is an arbitrary reference scale. The auxiliary
potential satisfies the linear equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GS-operator}
\mathcal L\mathcal F=0,\qquad
\mathcal L:=\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}.
\end{equation}
Replacing $\eta_{*}$ by another positive reference scale changes $\mathcal F$ only by a constant multiple of $r^{2}$. Since $\mathcal L(r^{2})=0$, this freedom leaves the field equation $\mathcal L\mathcal F=0$ unchanged. The derivative of the transformation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Phi} with respect to $\eta$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-eta-A}
\partial_{\eta}\Phi_{\ellzero}(r,\eta)=2+\frac{\ellzero r^{2}}{\eta}-\ellzero\eta=2+\ellzero r\left(\frac{1}{v}-v\right)=:A_{\ellzero}(r,v).
\end{equation}
Thus, at fixed $r$, the Jacobian relating $\eta$ to the auxiliary
potential $\mathcal F$ is precisely the same factor that multiplies the
second-order derivatives in the exact VFE. The nonvanishing of $A_{\ellzero}$ is precisely the condition that allows the transformation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Phi} to be inverted locally with respect to $\eta$. This observation leads to the following local existence result for an exactly flat equatorial trace.

\begin{theorem}[Local analytic realization of an exactly flat trace]
\label{thm:local-flat-trace-existence}
Let $r_{*}>0$, $v_{0}\in(0,1)$, and $\ellzero\in\mathbb R$, and assume
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noncharacteristic-flat-trace}
A_{*}:=A_{\ellzero}(r_{*},v_{0})=2+\ellzero r_{*}
\left(\frac{1}{v_{0}}-v_{0}\right)\neq 0.
\end{equation}
Then, there exist $\delta,\varepsilon>0$, with $\delta<r_{*}$, and a
unique real-analytic solution of the exact constant-$\ell$ VFE on $(r_{*}-\delta,r_{*}+\delta)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ satisfying the Cauchy data $v(r,0)=v_{0}$, $v_{z}(r,0)=0$, and $r\in(r_{*}-\delta,r_{*}+\delta)$. The solution is even in $z$ and need not be spatially constant. Its leading transverse correction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-flat-trace-expansion}
v(r,z)=v_{0}+\frac{v_{0}-\ellzero r}{r^{2}\left[2+\ellzero r(1/v_{0}-v_{0})\right]}z^{2}+O(z^{4}).
\end{equation}
For every choice of $C>0$, of the additive integration constant in
$\Omega$, and of the value $\mu(r_{*},0)$, the field $\eta=rv$ lifts
uniquely to a real-analytic local metric of the exact $(\eta,H)$
rotating-dust family, with $H(\eta)=-C e^{\ellzero\eta}$. If, in addition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-density-at-rstar}
-\frac{2v_{0}}{1-v_{0}^{2}}<\ellzero r_{*}<\frac{2v_{0}}{1+v_{0}^{2}},
\end{equation}
then the neighbourhood can be chosen sufficiently small that $0<v<1$ and $\rho>0$ hold throughout it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first solve the linear equation for the auxiliary potential $\mathcal F$. Along $z=0$, we prescribe the analytic Cauchy data
\begin{align}
\mathcal F(r,0)&:=\Phi_{\ellzero}(r,rv_{0})=2rv_{0}+\ellzero r^{2}
\log\!\left(\frac{rv_{0}}{\eta_{*}}\right)-\frac{\ellzero}{2}r^{2}v_{0}^{2},
\label{eq:F-flat-boundary-data}\\
\mathcal F_{z}(r,0)&:=0.\label{eq:F-flat-normal-data}
\end{align}
Since $r_{*}>0$ and $v_{0}>0$, these data are analytic in a
neighbourhood of $r=r_{*}$. Moreover, Eq.~\eqref{eq:GS-operator} can be
written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_{zz}=-\mathcal F_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}\mathcal F_{r},
\end{equation}
whose coefficients are analytic near $(r_{*},0)$. The surface $z=0$ is
therefore noncharacteristic, and the Cauchy--Kowalevski theorem yields a
unique real-analytic solution $\mathcal F$ in a neighbourhood of $(r_{*},0)$ ( see, for example, Ref.~\cite{Evans2010PDE}). Because both the equation and the Cauchy data are invariant under $z\mapsto-z$, uniqueness implies $\mathcal F(r,z)=\mathcal F(r,-z)$. At $(r_{*},0)$, the condition
Eq.~\eqref{eq:noncharacteristic-flat-trace} is precisely $\partial_{\eta}\Phi_{\ellzero}(r_{*},r_{*}v_{0})=A_{\ellzero}(r_{*},v_{0})
\neq 0$. By continuity, after restricting to a sufficiently small neighbourhood,
$\partial_{\eta}\Phi_{\ellzero}$ remains nonzero. The analytic implicit-function theorem then provides a unique real-analytic field $\eta(r,z)$ satisfying $\mathcal F(r,z)=\Phi_{\ellzero}(r,\eta(r,z))$. On $z=0$, the prescribed data and local uniqueness give $\eta(r,0)=rv_{0}$, and hence, $v(r,0)=\eta(r,0)/r=v_{0}$. The evenness of $\mathcal F$ and uniqueness of the implicit solution imply that $\eta$, and therefore $v=\eta/r$, is even in $z$. In particular, $v_{z}(r,0)=0$. Since the transformation Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Phi} recasts the constant-$\ell$ field equation as $\mathcal L\mathcal F=0$, the resulting field $v$ satisfies the exact constant-$\ell$ VFE. We now verify that this VFE solution determines a full local Einstein--dust metric. Let us fix $C>0$ and the additive integration constant in $\Omega$. Moreover, we prescribe a value $\mu_{*}$ at $(r_{*},0)$. After
shrinking the neighbourhood if necessary, one has $\eta>0$ and
$0<v<1$. Let us set $H(\eta)=-C e^{\ellzero\eta}$ and $d\Omega/d\eta=H_{,\eta}/(2\eta)$. Further, define the Killing block $G=(g_{AB})$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-metric-components}. Because $\eta$ stays away from
zero on this patch, $H$, $\Omega$, and all entries of $G$ are
real analytic. Moreover, $g_{\phi\phi}=(-H)^{-1}(r^{2}-\eta^{2})>0$ and $\det G=-r^{2}<0$, so the Killing block has Lorentzian signature. It remains to reconstruct the meridional conformal factor. Let $M_{r}$ and $M_{z}$ denote the right-hand sides of the exact quadratures
\begin{align}
M_{r}:={}&\frac{1}{2r}\Bigl[
(g_{tt})_{r}(g_{\phi\phi})_{r}
-(g_{tt})_{z}(g_{\phi\phi})_{z}
-(g_{t\phi})_{r}^{2}+(g_{t\phi})_{z}^{2}
\Bigr],\label{eq:local-mu-quadrature-r}\\
M_{z}:={}&\frac{1}{2r}\Bigl[
(g_{tt})_{z}(g_{\phi\phi})_{r}
+(g_{tt})_{r}(g_{\phi\phi})_{z}
-2(g_{t\phi})_{r}(g_{t\phi})_{z}
\Bigr].\label{eq:local-mu-quadrature-z}
\end{align}
The Winicour construction summarized in
Eqs.~(2.5), (2.9), and (2.10) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} shows that, after the Killing block is expressed through $(\eta,H,\Omega)$, the compatibility condition $\partial_{z}M_{r}=\partial_{r}M_{z}$ is precisely the remaining Einstein-equation integrability condition, and it follows from $\mathcal L\mathcal F=0$. Thus, the analytic
one-form $M_{r}\,dr+M_{z}\,dz$ is closed. On a sufficiently small
rectangular neighbourhood, the analytic Poincar\'e lemma therefore gives a unique real-analytic function $\mu$ satisfying $\mu_{r}=M_{r}$, $\mu_{z}=M_{z}$, and $\mu(r_{*},0)=\mu_{*}$. Hence, the metric associated to the line element $ds^{2}=G_{AB}\,dx^{A}dx^{B}+e^{\mu}(dr^{2}+dz^{2})$ is a real-analytic Lorentzian metric of the exact rotating-dust family.
Once the constants just specified are fixed, the reconstruction is
unique. We may now choose $\delta,\varepsilon>0$, with $\delta<r_{*}$, so that the construction is valid on $(r_{*}-\delta,r_{*}+\delta)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$. Along the equatorial trace, we have $v_{r}(r,0)=v_{rr}(r,0)=v_{z}(r,0)=0$. Substitution into the exact constant-$\ell$ VFE, Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-constant-l}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-flat-trace-balance}
\left[2+\ellzero r\left(\frac{1}{v_{0}}-v_{0}\right)\right]v_{zz}(r,0)+\frac{2\ellzero}{r}-\frac{2v_{0}}{r^{2}}=0.
\end{equation}
Since the coefficient of $v_{zz}$ remains nonzero on a sufficiently
small radial interval around $r_{*}$, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-flat-trace-curvature}
v_{zz}(r,0)=\frac{2(v_{0}-\ellzero r)}{r^{2}\left[2+\ellzero r(1/v_{0}-v_{0})
\right]}.
\end{equation}
Because $v$ is even in $z$, its Taylor expansion about the equatorial
plane therefore gives
\begin{equation}
v(r,z)=v_{0}+\frac{v_{0}-\ellzero r}{r^{2}\left[2+\ellzero r(1/v_{0}-v_{0})
\right]}z^{2}+O(z^{4}),
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-flat-trace-expansion}. Finally, suppose that the strict inequalities Eq.~\eqref{eq:strict-density-at-rstar} hold. At $(r_{*},0)$ we have $\eta_{r}=v_{0}\neq 0$ and $\eta_{z}=0$, so the point is noncritical and the algebraic density factor is strictly positive by Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain}. The reconstructed metric has $g_{rr}=e^{\mu}>0$, with $\mu$ finite and analytic, while $\eta>0$ on the chosen patch. The exact density formula therefore gives $\rho(r_{*},0)>0$. Since every factor entering that formula is continuous, the neighbourhood can be reduced once more so that $0<v<1$ and $\rho>0$ hold throughout it.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Regular-axis obstruction for nonzero constant-\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} branches}

The preceding construction establishes local analytic solutions on patches
separated from the rotation axis. We now ask whether a nontrivial
constant-$\ell$ branch can be extended regularly to the axis when
$\ellzero\neq 0$. Recall that $k=\partial_{t}$ and $m=\partial_{\phi}$ denote the stationary and axial Killing fields. A regular segment of the rotation axis lies in the fixed-point set of the $U(1)$ action generated by $m$. In local
Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ adapted to this action, the axial Killing
field takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-generator-cartesian}
m=-y\,\partial_{x}+x\,\partial_{y},\qquad
\mathcal A=\{x=y=0\}.
\end{equation}

The obstruction is already contained in the metric jets; regular extension
of the material congruence is not needed.

\begin{theorem}[Metric-only axis-jet obstruction]
\label{thm:metric-axis-jet-obstruction}
Let a $C^2$ stationary Lorentz metric extend across a fixed component
$\mathcal A$ of an effective isometric $U(1)$ action of period $2\pi$, and
let the stationary Killing field $k$ commute with its axial generator $m$.
Assume that a nontrivial $(\eta,H)$ branch is connected to $\mathcal A$,
that $k$ is timelike on $\mathcal A$, that $0<|\eta|<r$ on the punctured
branch with $\eta\to0$ at $\mathcal A$, that $H$ is $C^1$ at $\eta=0$
with $H(0)=-C<0$, and that the exact
relations in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:model-H-eta-definitions}--\eqref{eq:model-metric-components}
hold off the axis. Then
$$
H'(0)=0.
$$
Consequently, a constant logarithmic derivative $H'/H=\ellzero$ on the
connected branch must satisfy $\ellzero=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\varrho$ be a smooth transverse distance to the axis. In a smooth
invariant frame the axis jets are
\begin{equation}
g(m,m)=a\varrho^2+o(\varrho^2),\qquad
g(k,m)=O(\varrho^2),\qquad
g(k,k)=-D+O(\varrho^2),
\label{eq:metric-axis-jets}
\end{equation}
with $a>0$ and $D>0$. The determinant identity then gives
\begin{equation}
r^2=Da\varrho^2+o(\varrho^2),
\label{eq:axis-r-rho-comparison}
\end{equation}
so $r\asymp\varrho$ and $g(m,m)/r^2\to1/D$.

Suppose, for contradiction, that $h_1:=H'(0)\neq0$. The exact relation
$\Omega_{,\eta}=H_{,\eta}/(2\eta)$ gives
$$
\Omega(\eta)=\frac{h_1}{2}\log|\eta|
+o\!\left(\lvert\log|\eta|\rvert\right),
$$
and therefore $|\Omega|\to\infty$. From
$\eta=g(k,m)+\Omega g(m,m)$ and
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:metric-axis-jets}--\eqref{eq:axis-r-rho-comparison},
$$
\frac{\eta}{r^2\Omega}\longrightarrow\frac1D.
$$
Writing $y=|\log|\eta||$, the resulting lower comparison
$|\eta|\geqslant c r^2|\Omega|\geqslant c' r^2y$ gives
$y+\log y\leqslant2|\log r|+O(1)$. Thus
$|\Omega|=O(|\log r|)$, $r^2\Omega^2\to0$, and
$\Omega\eta\to0$. The exact formula for $g(k,k)$ now gives
$g(k,k)|_{\mathcal A}=H(0)=-C$, so $D=C$. Moreover,
$H+C=O(|\eta|)=O(r^2|\Omega|)$. Hence
$$
\frac{H+C}{r^2\Omega^2}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{g(k,m)}{r^2\Omega}\longrightarrow0.
$$
On the one hand, metric regularity gives
$(g(k,k)+C)/(r^2\Omega^2)\to0$. On the other hand, substituting
$g(k,k)=H-2\Omega\eta+\Omega^2g(m,m)$ and then
$\eta=g(k,m)+\Omega g(m,m)$ yields
\begin{align}
\frac{g(k,k)+C}{r^2\Omega^2}
={}&\frac{H+C}{r^2\Omega^2}
-\frac{2g(k,m)}{r^2\Omega}
-\frac{g(m,m)}{r^2}
\longrightarrow-\frac1C,
\end{align}
a contradiction. Thus $H'(0)=0$. For
$H=-Ce^{\ellzero\eta}$, one has $H'(0)=-C\ellzero$, proving the final
assertion.
\end{proof}

The proof uses the full quadratic axis jets rather than elementary
flatness alone. This distinction is sharp: elementary-flat punctured
metrics of lower regularity need not satisfy the conclusion.

The following lemma records the local regularity property of azimuthal
vector fields that will be needed in the axis analysis.

\begin{lemma}[Regularity of an azimuthal coefficient at the axis]
\label{lem:smooth-azimuthal-coefficient}
Let $X=\omega(x,y,z)m$ be an axisymmetric vector field defined on a
punctured neighbourhood of a regular axis segment. If $X$ extends as a
$C^{1}$ vector field across the axis, then $\omega$ has a finite limit
as the axis is approached.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let us consider first the half-line $y=0$, $x>0$. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:axis-generator-cartesian}, we have $X^{y}(x,0,z)=x\,\omega(x,0,z)$. The continuous axisymmetric extension of the purely azimuthal field $X$ must vanish on the axis. In particular, we find $X^{y}(0,0,z)=0$. For $x>0$, we have
\begin{equation}
\omega(x,0,z)=\frac{X^{y}(x,0,z)-X^{y}(0,0,z)}{x}.
\end{equation}
Since $X^{y}$ is differentiable at the axis, taking $x\downarrow 0$ gives
\begin{equation}
\lim_{x\downarrow0}\omega(x,0,z)=\partial_{x}X^{y}(0,0,z),
\end{equation}
which is finite. Axisymmetry implies that $\omega$ depends only on the radial distance from the axis and on $z$, so the same limit is obtained from any radial direction.
\end{proof}

The preceding lemma provides the regularity condition needed to test
whether a constant-$\ell$ branch can approach the rotation axis. The
potential obstruction comes from the angular velocity $\Omega(\eta)$.
For real $x\neq 0$, the principal-value exponential integral has the
small-argument expansion
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Ei}(x)=\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\log|x|+O(x),
\qquad x\to 0,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\mathrm E}$ is the Euler--Mascheroni constant (see Eqs.~6.2.6--6.2.7 in \cite{NISTDLMF}). Accordingly, for $\ellzero\neq 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Omega} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-l-omega-axis}
\Omega(\eta)=-\frac{C\ellzero}{2}\log|\eta|+O(1)
\end{equation}
as $\eta\to 0$ with $\eta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. Thus, $\Omega$ diverges logarithmically as a nonzero constant-$\ell$ branch approaches $\eta=0$ from either side. The following theorem shows that this behaviour is incompatible with a regular extension to the rotation axis.

\begin{theorem}[Regular-axis no-go for nonzero constant-$\ell$ branches]
\label{thm:regular-axis-constant-l-no-go}
Let an exact stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust solution of the
$(\eta,H)$ family be defined on a connected invariant region
$\mathcal U$ whose closure meets a regular segment $\mathcal A$ of the
rotation axis. Assume that
\begin{enumerate}
\item
the metric extends across $\mathcal A$ as a $C^{2}$ Lorentzian
metric, the stationary Killing field is nonzero on
$\mathcal A$, and the axial Killing field generates closed orbits with
angular period $2\pi$;
\item
the dust four-velocity extends as a $C^{1}$ timelike vector field
to every regular limit point of the branch in $\overline{\mathcal U}$;
\item
$|v|=|\eta|/r<1$ away from the axis and $H<0$;
\item
the logarithmic derivative $H_{,\eta}/H$ is constant on the
connected range of $\eta$ attained in $\mathcal U$, with
$H_{,\eta}/H\equiv\ellzero$;
\item
the branch is nontrivial, so that $\eta\not\equiv 0$ on $\mathcal U$.
\end{enumerate}
Then necessarily $\ellzero=0$. This conclusion does not depend on the spatial form of $v(r,z)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The subluminal condition $|v|<1$, together with $\eta=rv$, gives
$|\eta|<r$ away from the axis. Since the orbit-area radius tends to zero at a regular axis segment, $\eta$ tends to zero along every approach to $\mathcal A$ from $\mathcal U$. Let us set $\mathcal Z:=\{\eta\neq0\}\cap\mathcal U$. The branch is nontrivial, so $\mathcal Z$ is nonempty. Moreover, let us choose a connected component $\mathcal U_{*}$ of $\mathcal Z$. If $\mathcal U_{*}=\mathcal U$, then its closure meets $\mathcal A$, and there is a sequence $p_{j}\in\mathcal U_{*}$ approaching a regular axis
point. If $\mathcal U_{*}$ is a proper subset of $\mathcal U$, then the connectedness of $\mathcal U$ implies that its relative boundary
$\partial_{\mathcal U}\mathcal U_{*}$ is nonempty. Choose
$q\in\partial_{\mathcal U}\mathcal U_{*}$. Continuity forces
$\eta(q)=0$ because otherwise $q$ would lie in the open set $\mathcal Z$ and in the same connected component as nearby points of $\mathcal U_{*}$. Thus, in either case, there is a sequence $p_{j}\in\mathcal U_{*}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-sequence-to-zero}
\eta(p_{j})\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $p_{j}$ approaches either a regular axis point or a regular
interior point $q\in\mathcal U$ with $\eta(q)=0$. On $\mathcal U_{*}$ the field $\eta$ has a fixed sign, and the dust
four-velocity may be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u-axis-decomposition}
u=N(k+\Omega m),\qquad N:=(-H)^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
For constant $\ell=\ellzero$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-H} and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta-sequence-to-zero} give
\begin{equation}
N(p_{j})\longrightarrow N_{0}:=C^{-1/2}\in(0,\infty).
\end{equation}
If $\ellzero\neq0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-omega-axis} gives
$|\Omega(p_{j})|\to\infty$. Suppose first that $p_{j}\to q$ for a regular interior point with $r(q)>0$. The identity $\det G=-r^{2}<0$ implies that $k$ and $m$ are linearly independent near $q$. After shrinking to a neighbourhood of $q$, we choose a smooth one-form $\vartheta$ dual to the axial direction in the sense that $\vartheta(k)=0$, and $\vartheta(m)=1$. Then, $\vartheta(u)=N\Omega$
diverges along $p_{j}$, whereas both $u$ and $\vartheta$ extend
continuously to $q$. This contradiction excludes an interior limit
point with $\eta=0$ when $\ellzero\neq 0$. It remains to consider a sequence approaching the regular axis. The local product structure of the commuting stationary and axial actions, together with the nonvanishing of $k$ on $\mathcal A$, provides a smooth one-form $\tau$ in a neighbourhood of the axis satisfying $\tau(k)=1$ and $\tau(m)=0$. Equivalently, in a smooth chart adapted to both actions we may take $\tau=dt$. Unlike $d\phi$, this one-form is regular on the axis. Applying $\tau$ to Eq.~\eqref{eq:u-axis-decomposition} gives $N=\tau(u)$ on the punctured branch. Because $u$ extends as a $C^{1}$ vector field and $\tau$ is smooth, $N$ extends as a $C^{1}$ scalar to the axis. Therefore, $Nk$ is $C^{1}$, and the azimuthal part $X:=u-Nk=N\Omega m$ extends as a $C^{1}$ axisymmetric vector field. By Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-azimuthal-coefficient}, the coefficient $N\Omega$ has a finite axis limit. Since $N\to N_{0}\neq 0$, the angular velocity $\Omega$ must also have a finite limit, in contradiction with Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-omega-axis}. Both possible limits of the sequence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta-sequence-to-zero} are therefore incompatible with $\ellzero\neq 0$. Hence, $\ellzero=0$.
\end{proof}

The regular-axis condition also has a simple interpretation at the level
of $H(\eta)$. If the stationary time coordinate is normalized so that
$H(0)=-1$, regularity requires the linear term in an analytic expansion
of $H$ about $\eta=0$ to vanish, i.e. $H(\eta)=-1+O(\eta^{2})$. The same coefficient condition is obtained independently in Ref.~\cite{Galoppo2025EPJC} by imposing regularity of the axis together with vanishing matter rotation there. By contrast, a nonzero constant
$\ellzero$ gives
\begin{equation}
H(\eta)=-e^{\ellzero\eta}=-1-\ellzero\eta+O(\eta^{2}),
\end{equation}
after the normalization $H(0)=-1$, and therefore produces precisely the
excluded linear term. Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-axis-constant-l-no-go}
provides an independent congruence-level proof without assuming analyticity
of $H$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-axis-jet-obstruction} removes the
congruence hypothesis altogether. Thus, unless one of the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-axis-jet-obstruction} fails, a nonzero
constant-$\ell$ branch can represent only an annular or piecewise
region. The remaining value $\ellzero=0$ is compatible with a regular
axis, and its axis behaviour is determined next.

\begin{lemma}[Axis behaviour of the $\ellzero=0$ branch]
\label{lem:rigid-axis-v-zero}
Under the regular-axis hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-axis-constant-l-no-go}, let $\ellzero=0$. Then, in the orbit-area radial coordinate $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-axis-v-decay}
\eta=O(r^{2}),\qquad
v=\frac{\eta}{r}=O(r)
\end{equation}
as $r\to 0$. In particular, $v\to 0$ as any regular segment of the rotation axis is approached.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\ellzero=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-H} gives $H=-C<0$, while
Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-velocity-relations} implies that the coordinate
angular velocity is a finite constant, $\Omega=\Omega_{0}$. The metric relation for the mixed Killing component therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-from-axis-metric}
\eta=g_{t\phi}+\Omega_{0}g_{\phi\phi}.
\end{equation}
Let $\varrho=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$ denote the local cylindrical distance from the axis in the Cartesian coordinates introduced above. Smoothness and axisymmetry imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-axis-metric-orders}
g_{\phi\phi}=O(\varrho^{2}),\qquad
g_{t\phi}=O(\varrho^{2})
\end{equation}
as $\varrho\to 0$. Indeed, the axial Killing field $m=-y\partial_x+x\partial_y$ vanishes linearly at the axis, and the regular axisymmetric metric coefficients involving $m$ therefore vanish quadratically. It remains to relate $\varrho$ to the orbit-area coordinate $r$. The exact identity $g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}-g_{t\phi}^{2}=-r^{2}$ together with regularity and nondegeneracy of the stationary direction at the axis shows that $r$ vanishes linearly with the local cylindrical distance $\varrho$. Hence, $r=O(\varrho)$, and $\varrho=O(r)$
for $\varrho\to 0$. Combining this relation with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:eta-from-axis-metric} and \eqref{eq:smooth-axis-metric-orders} gives $\eta=O(\varrho^{2})=O(r^{2})$. Since $v=\eta/r$, it follows immediately that $v=O(r)$, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-axis-v-decay}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Global obstruction to axis-connected exactly flat traces}

The regular-axis theorem excludes every nonzero constant-$\ell$ branch
from extending regularly to the rotation axis. It remains to examine the
only admissible constant value, $\ellzero=0$. In this case, analyticity
away from the axis implies that an exactly flat equatorial trace on any
open radial interval propagates across the entire connected equatorial
domain. The resulting behaviour is incompatible with the axis decay
established in Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid-axis-v-zero}. This gives the following
global obstruction, without requiring any assumption on the sign of
$v_{zz}$.

\begin{theorem}[No axis-connected exactly flat constant-$\ell$ trace]
\label{thm:all-constant-l-flat-trace-no-go}
Let a nontrivial exact constant-$\ell$ solution satisfy the regular-axis
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-axis-constant-l-no-go}. Define its
equatorial radial domain by $\mathcal I_{0}:=\{r>0:(r,0)\in\mathcal U\}$. Assume that $\mathcal I_{0}$ is a connected open interval with
$0\in\overline{\mathcal I_{0}}$, so that the equatorial part of the branch extends continuously toward the regular axis without an intervening junction, singularity, or gap. If there exist a nonempty open interval
$I\subset\mathcal I_{0}$ and a constant $v_{0}\in(0,1)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-flat-trace-data}
v(r,0)=v_{0}
\end{equation}
for all $r\in I$, then no such solution exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-axis-constant-l-no-go} first implies $\ellzero=0$. For this value, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Phi} reduces to $\mathcal F=2\eta$, up to an irrelevant additive solution of the linear equation. Equation~\eqref{eq:GS-operator} therefore becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-eta-equation}
\eta_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}\eta_{r}+\eta_{zz}=0
\end{equation}
with $r>0$. Away from the axis, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-eta-equation} is a linear
elliptic equation with real-analytic coefficients. Interior analytic
regularity therefore implies that $\eta$ is real analytic on every neighbourhood contained in $r>0$ \cite{MorreyNirenberg1957CPAM}. Since $v=\eta/r$, the function $r\mapsto v(r,0)$ is consequently real analytic on the connected interval $\mathcal I_{0}$. By assumption, $v(r,0)-v_{0}=0$ for all $r\in I$, on a nonempty open subinterval $I\subset\mathcal I_{0}$. The
one-dimensional identity theorem for real-analytic functions therefore
extends this equality to the whole connected interval, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-trace-analytic-propagation}
v(r,0)\equiv v_{0}
\end{equation}
for all $r\in\mathcal I_{0}$. Because $0$ is in the closure of $\mathcal I_{0}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:flat-trace-analytic-propagation} implies $v(r,0)\longrightarrow v_{0}>0$ as $r\downarrow 0$. On the other hand, Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid-axis-v-zero} requires $v(r,0)=O(r)\longrightarrow 0$ as $r\downarrow 0$ at a regular axis. The two limits are incompatible, and the assumed axis-connected solution therefore cannot exist.
\end{proof}

The connectedness assumption in Theorem~\ref{thm:all-constant-l-flat-trace-no-go} ensures that the equatorial domain belongs to a single axis-connected branch. In particular, it excludes an intervening vacuum region, junction,
singular ring, or other gap between the rotation axis and the flat
annulus. The result does not rely on reflection symmetry, density
positivity, or any assumption on the sign of the vertical curvature. Moreover, there is no conflict with Theorem~\ref{thm:local-flat-trace-existence}, which establishes the existence of local solutions on annular patches away from the axis. The global theorem shows instead that such a patch cannot be continued
as a single regular constant-$\ell$ branch to the rotation axis while
preserving an exactly flat equatorial trace on any open radial interval. For the remaining case $\ellzero=0$, the local continuation of flat,
reflection-symmetric Cauchy data can be obtained explicitly. This also
reveals the transverse behaviour responsible for the failure of a
globally regular flat trace.

\begin{corollary}[Explicit $\ellzero=0$ solution with an exactly flat trace]
\label{cor:explicit-rigid-flat-continuation}
Let $\ellzero=0$ and $r_{*}>0$. Consider the reflection-symmetric Cauchy
data $v(r,0)=v_{0}$ and $v_{z}(r,0)=0$, with $0<v_{0}<1$, for $r$ in a
neighbourhood of $r_{*}$. The unique local analytic solution of the exact
constant-$\ell$ VFE is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-rigid-flat-solution}
v(r,z)=v_{0}\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}+z^{2}}}{r}.
\end{equation}
Along the equatorial plane,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-flat-trace-minimum}
v_{zz}(r,0)=\frac{v_{0}}{r^{2}}>0,
\end{equation}
so $z=0$ is a strict local minimum of $v$ in the vertical direction. If
the explicit solution is continued at fixed $r>0$ along the vertical
direction, it reaches $v=1$ at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigid-luminal-height}
|z|=r\sqrt{v_{0}^{-2}-1},
\end{equation}
and becomes superluminal for larger $|z|$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $\ellzero=0$, the field $\eta=rv$ satisfies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-eta-equation}. Consider $\eta_{0}(r,z):=v_{0}\sqrt{r^{2}+z^{2}}$. To verify that $\eta_{0}$ is a solution, let
$R=\sqrt{r^{2}+z^{2}}$ and suppose that a function of $(r,z)$ depends
only on $R$, so that it can be written as $f=f(R)$. A direct calculation
gives
\begin{equation}
f_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}f_{r}+f_{zz}=f''(R).
\end{equation}
For $f(R)=v_{0}R$, one has $f''(R)=0$, and therefore $\eta_{0}$ satisfies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-eta-equation} wherever $r>0$. On the equatorial plane,
$\eta_{0}(r,0)=rv_{0}$ and $(\eta_{0})_{z}(r,0)=0$, which are precisely
the Cauchy data in the statement of the corollary. Analytic uniqueness
near $(r_{*},0)$ therefore gives $\eta=\eta_{0}$, and hence
\begin{equation}
v(r,z)=\frac{\eta(r,z)}{r}
=v_{0}\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}+z^{2}}}{r},
\end{equation}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:explicit-rigid-flat-solution}. Differentiation
with respect to $z$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-flat-trace-minimum}.
Finally, solving $v(r,z)=1$ at fixed $r>0$ yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigid-luminal-height}. Since $v$ increases with $|z|$ away
from the equatorial plane, it exceeds unity beyond that height.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Finite radial bounds for annular flat traces}

We now ask how far an exactly flat trace can extend in such an annular
branch under nonnegative density and a local vertical-maximum
condition, without otherwise restricting the off-plane profile.

\begin{theorem}[Finite radial window for an exactly flat annular trace]
\label{thm:annular-flat-trace-bound}
Let an exact constant-$\ell$ solution be $C^{2}$ on $I\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$, where $I\subset(0,\infty)$ is a nonempty open interval. Further assume that
\begin{enumerate}
\item
the equatorial trace is exactly flat, $v(r,0)=v_{0}$ with $0<v_{0}<1$ for all $r\in I$;
\item
the density is nonnegative along the trace, $\rho(r,0)\geqslant 0$ for all $r\in I$;
\item
for each $r\in I$, the equatorial plane is a local maximum of $v$ in the vertical direction.
\end{enumerate}
Then, $\ellzero>0$, and every $r\in I$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annular-radius-window}
\frac{v_{0}}{\ellzero}\leqslant r\leqslant\frac{2v_{0}}
{\ellzero(1+v_{0}^{2})}.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, $\rho(r,0)>0$, and $v_{zz}(r,0)<0$ for all $r\in I$, then both inequalities in Eq.~\eqref{eq:annular-radius-window} are strict.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along the exactly flat trace, $\eta(r,0)=rv_{0}$ and $\eta_{r}(r,0)=v_{0}$. Since $z=0$ is a local vertical maximum and $v$ is $C^{2}$, $v_{z}(r,0)=0$, and hence $\eta_{z}(r,0)=0$. Therefore, $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$ throughout the trace, and Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain} applies directly. Let us set $x:=\ellzero r$. Nonnegative density then requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-trace-x-domain}
x_{-}:=-\frac{2v_{0}}{1-v_{0}^{2}}\leqslant x\leqslant x_{+}:=\frac{2v_{0}}{1+v_{0}^{2}}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $v_{zz}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-flat-trace-balance} can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-of-x-flat-trace}
A(x):=2+\frac{1-v_{0}^{2}}{v_{0}}x.
\end{equation}
At the lower density endpoint $x=x_{-}$, one has $A(x_{-})=0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-flat-trace-balance} would then reduce to $2(x_{-}-v_{0})/r^{2}=0$, which is impossible because $x_{-}<0<v_{0}$. Hence, $x=x_{-}$ cannot occur on a $C^{2}$ flat trace. It follows that $x>x_{-}$ and $A(x)>0$ hold throughout $I$. Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-flat-trace-curvature} may therefore be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vzz-x-form}
v_{zz}(r,0)=\frac{2(v_{0}-x)}{r^{2}A(x)}.
\end{equation}
Because $A(x)>0$, the condition $v_{zz}(r,0)\leqslant 0$ implies
$x\geqslant v_{0}$. Since $v_{0}>0$ and $r>0$, this gives $\ellzero>0$. Combining $x\geqslant v_{0}$ with the upper density bound $x\leqslant 2v_{0}/(1+v_{0}^{2})$ and using $x=\ellzero r$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:annular-radius-window}. Finally, if $\rho(r,0)>0$, the upper density bound is strict, so $x<x_{+}$. If $v_{zz}(r,0)<0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:vzz-x-form} gives $x>v_{0}$. The two radial inequalities are therefore strict.
\end{proof}

An immediate consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-flat-trace-bound} is a bound on the radial span of the flat trace. For any two radii $r_{1}<r_{2}$ in $I$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:annular-radius-window} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-trace-span-ratio}
\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\leqslant\frac{2}{1+v_{0}^{2}}<2,
\end{equation}
where the final inequality follows from $0<v_{0}<1$. Thus, irrespective
of its off-plane structure, a constant-$\ell$ solution satisfying the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-flat-trace-bound} cannot support
an exactly flat equatorial trace over a radial factor of two or more.


\section{Quantitative bounds for approximately flat equatorial traces}
\label{sec:approx-flat-obstruction}

The preceding results concern equatorial traces that are exactly flat.
We now relax this condition and ask how slowly the equatorial speed may
vary while remaining compatible with the exact constant-$\ell$ field
equation, nonnegative density, and vertical localization. The strategy
is to measure deviations from flatness by the logarithmic slope of the
equatorial trace, reduce the exact VFE on the equatorial plane to a
one-dimensional equation, and use the resulting differential
inequalities to constrain the radial interval over which approximate
flatness can persist.


\subsection{Exact reduction on the equatorial plane}

We begin by introducing a dimensionless measure of approximate flatness.
Because logarithmic radial derivatives are invariant under a rescaling
of the radial coordinate, they provide a natural measure of the
variation of a rotation curve across a finite radial interval.

\begin{definition}[$\varepsilon$-flat equatorial trace]
\label{def:epsilon-flat}
Let $0<R_{1}<R_{2}$. We define the logarithmic radial span as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-span}
\Lambda:=\log\!\left(\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\right).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, let $u(r):=v(r,0)$ be a positive $C^{1}$ equatorial trace on $[R_{1},R_{2}]$. For $0\leqslant\varepsilon<1$, we say that $u$ is $\varepsilon$-flat if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon-flat-definition}
\left|\frac{d\log u}{d\log r}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon
\end{equation}
throughout $[R_{1},R_{2}]$.
\end{definition}

The case $\varepsilon=0$ corresponds to exact flatness, whereas
$\varepsilon>0$ permits a slowly rising or slowly falling trace. The
restriction $\varepsilon<1$ will also be useful below. Indeed, if $s:=d\log u/d\log r$, then
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\log(ru)}{d\log r}=1+s\geqslant1-\varepsilon>0.
\end{equation}
Hence, $\eta(r,0)=ru(r)$ is strictly increasing along an
$\varepsilon$-flat trace, so the critical-point degeneracy of the
density formula cannot occur there. To reduce the equatorial field equation, we introduce the dimensionless logarithmic radial coordinate
\begin{equation}
t:=\log\!\left(\frac{r}{R_{1}}\right), \qquad
0\leqslant t\leqslant\Lambda.
\end{equation}
For a sufficiently smooth trace, we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-flat-variables}
s:=\frac{d\log u}{dt},\qquad
q:=\frac{ds}{dt},\qquad
y:=\frac{\ellzero r}{u},\qquad
Z:=\frac{r^{2}v_{zz}(r,0)}{u}.
\end{equation}
Since $dt=d\log r$, the $\varepsilon$-flatness condition is simply $|s|\leqslant\varepsilon$. Using $d/dr=r^{-1}d/dt$, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-flat-derivative-identities}
u_{r}=\frac{us}{r},\qquad
u_{rr}=\frac{u}{r^{2}}\left(q+s^{2}-s\right),\qquad
\frac{dy}{dt}=(1-s)y.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic variables introduced above allow the exact constant-$\ell$
VFE to be reduced directly on the equatorial plane. The resulting identity
also makes it possible to incorporate the density-positivity and vertical-
localization conditions in a form suited to quantitative estimates.

\begin{lemma}[Exact logarithmic equatorial identity]
\label{lem:exact-equatorial-log-identity}
Let $v$ be a $C^{2}$ reflection-symmetric solution of the exact constant-$\ell$ VFE in a neighbourhood of $[R_{1},R_{2}]\times\{0\}$, and assume $0<u(r)<1$ for
$R_{1}\leqslant r\leqslant R_{2}$. In terms of the variables defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-variables}, the exact VFE restricted to the
equatorial plane becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-equatorial-log-identity}
\left[2+y(1-u^{2})\right](Z+q)+2(1-yu^{2})s^{2}+2y(1-u^{2})s+2y-2=0.
\end{equation}
If, in addition, the trace is $\varepsilon$-flat and $\rho(r,0)\geqslant 0$ throughout $[R_{1},R_{2}]$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-density-domain}
-\frac{2}{1-u^{2}}\leqslant y\leqslant\frac{2}{1+u^{2}}.
\end{equation}
Finally, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-approx-flat}
A:=2+y(1-u^{2}).
\end{equation}
At every point where $A>0$ and the equatorial plane is a local maximum
of $v$ in the vertical direction, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master-approx-flat-inequality}
Aq\geqslant 2(1-y)-2(1-yu^{2})s^{2}-2y(1-u^{2})s.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reflection symmetry implies $v_{z}(r,0)=0$. Substituting the derivative identities in Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-derivative-identities} into the exact
constant-$\ell$ VFE, evaluating at $z=0$, multiplying by $r^{2}/u$, and using $\ellzero r=yu$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-equatorial-log-identity} after collecting terms. Now suppose that the trace is $\varepsilon$-flat and that
$\rho(r,0)\geqslant 0$. Along the equatorial plane, we find $\eta_{r}(r,0)=u+ru_{r}=
u(1+s)$. Since $|s|\leqslant\varepsilon<1$ and $u>0$, we have $\eta_{r}(r,0)>0$. Together with reflection symmetry, which gives $\eta_{z}(r,0)=0$, this shows that $\nabla_{rz}\eta\neq 0$ throughout the trace. Hence,
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-positivity-domain} applies without the
critical-point qualification. Dividing its bounds for $x=r\ellzero$ by $u>0$ and using $y=x/u$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:y-density-domain}. Finally, Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-equatorial-log-identity} can be rearranged
as
\begin{equation}
Aq=2(1-y)-2(1-yu^{2})s^{2}-2y(1-u^{2})s-AZ.
\end{equation}
If $A>0$ and the equatorial plane is a local vertical maximum, then
$v_{zz}(r,0)\leqslant 0$. Since $u>0$, this is equivalent to $Z\leqslant 0$. Therefore, $-AZ\geqslant 0$, which gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:master-approx-flat-inequality}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Calibrated density-only span bound}

Before imposing any condition on the vertical curvature, the
density-positivity bound alone already constrains the radial span once
the strength of the constant-$\ell$ branch is fixed at the inner edge.
The following corollary makes this dependence explicit.

\begin{corollary}[Density-only span bound for a calibrated branch]
\label{cor:density-only-approx-flat-bound}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-equatorial-log-identity}, let us suppose that the equatorial trace is $\varepsilon$-flat, $\rho(r,0)\geqslant 0$ throughout $[R_{1},R_{2}]$, and $\ellzero>0$. No condition on the sign of $v_{zz}$ is imposed. Further, we define the dimensionless inner-edge parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-inner-rotation-strength}
\gamma_{1}:=\frac{\ellzero R_{1}}{u(R_{1})}>0
\end{equation}
and let $\underline v:=\displaystyle{\min_{[R_{1},R_{2}]}u}$.
Then the following inequality holds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-only-span-bound}
\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\leqslant\left[\frac{2}{\gamma_{1}(1+\underline v^{2})}
\right]^{1/(1-\varepsilon)}.
\end{equation}
For the edge calibration of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, that is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-edge-calibration-approx}
R_{1}=\RG,\qquad
\ellzero=\frac{a\vc}{\RG},\qquad
u(\RG)=a\vc,
\end{equation}
one has $\gamma_{1}=1$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paper-edge-approx-flat-bound}
\frac{R_{2}}{\RG}\leqslant\left(\frac{2}{1+\underline v^{2}}
\right)^{1/(1-\varepsilon)}<2^{1/(1-\varepsilon)}.
\end{equation}
For $\varepsilon=0$, this reduces to the exact density-positivity cutoff
of Corollary~\ref{cor:paper-density-cutoff}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\ellzero>0$ and $u>0$ for all $r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]$, the variable $y=(\ellzero r)/u$ is positive throughout $[R_{1},R_{2}]$. From
Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-derivative-identities}, we have
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dt}=1-s.
\end{equation}
Integrating from $t=0$, corresponding to $r=R_{1}$, to $t=\Lambda$, corresponding to $r=R_{2}$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-integrated-growth-density-only}
\log\!\left(\frac{y(R_{2})}{y(R_{1})}\right)=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}(1-s)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Because the trace is $\varepsilon$-flat, $|s|\leqslant\varepsilon$, and hence
\begin{equation}
\log\!\left(\frac{y(R_{2})}{y(R_{1})}\right)\geqslant (1-\varepsilon)\Lambda.
\end{equation}
The density bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:y-density-domain} gives
\begin{equation}
y(R_{2})\leqslant\frac{2}{1+u(R_{2})^{2}}\leqslant\frac{2}{1+\underline v^{2}},
\end{equation}
while $y(R_{1})=\gamma_{1}$. It follows that
\begin{equation}
(1-\varepsilon)\Lambda\leqslant\log\!\left[\frac{2}{(1+\underline v^{2})\gamma_{1}}
\right].
\end{equation}
Using $\Lambda=\log(R_{2}/R_{1})$ and exponentiating yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-only-span-bound}. For the calibration in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-edge-calibration-approx},
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{1}=\frac{(a\vc/\RG)\RG}{a\vc}=1,
\end{equation}
which gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-edge-approx-flat-bound}. When
$\varepsilon=0$, the trace is constant, so $\underline v=a\vc$, and the bound becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{R_{2}}{\RG}\leqslant\frac{2}{1+a^{2}\vc^{2}},
\end{equation}
in agreement with Eq.~\eqref{eq:paper-exact-density-cutoff}.
\end{proof}

The dependence on $\gamma_{1}$ is essential. Approximate flatness and
nonnegative density alone do not yield a parameter-free upper bound on
the radial span, because $\gamma_{1}$ may be chosen arbitrarily small.
This limitation is also visible in the explicit $\ellzero=0$ solution
of Corollary~\ref{cor:explicit-rigid-flat-continuation}, for which
$s\equiv 0$ and the density is positive on every compact annulus on which
the solution remains subluminal. In that example, however, the
equatorial plane is a vertical minimum. A condition controlling the transverse behaviour is therefore needed to obtain a universal finite-span estimate. In what follows, we impose only the weak pointwise localization condition $v_{zz}(r,0)\leqslant 0$, without assuming any bound on the logarithmic slope derivative
$q=ds/dt$.

\subsection{Universal radial-span bounds under vertical localization}

The localization condition has an invariant formulation. Let
$q=e^\mu(dr^2+dz^2)$ be the orbit metric and let
$r=\sqrt{-\det G}$ be the orbit-area scalar. On a reflection surface on
which $dr\neq0$, choose the unit transverse normal
$$
N=\frac{J\operatorname{grad}_q r}{|\operatorname{grad}_q r|_q},
$$
where $J$ is the orientation-compatible quarter turn. The relative
dust--ZAMO rapidity is the scalar
$\psi=\operatorname{artanh}v$.

\begin{proposition}[Invariant vertical-shape condition]
\label{prop:invariant-vertical-shape}
On a reflection surface, with the orientation of $N$ chosen as above,
\begin{equation}
N(N\psi)=e^{-\mu}\frac{v_{zz}}{1-v^2}.
\label{eq:invariant-vertical-shape}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $v_{zz}\leqslant0$ is equivalent to the invariant statement
$N(N\psi)\leqslant0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the Weyl chart one has $N=e^{-\mu/2}\partial_z$ on the reflection
surface. Reflection symmetry gives $v_z=\psi_z=0$, and hence
$$
N(N\psi)=e^{-\mu}\psi_{zz}
=e^{-\mu}\frac{v_{zz}}{1-v^2}.
$$
The factor multiplying $v_{zz}$ is positive, which proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}

This condition says that the relative rapidity has a transverse maximum.
It is not a statement that the density or total mass is localized there.

\begin{theorem}[Universal span bounds for approximately flat,
vertically localized traces]
\label{thm:quantitative-approx-flat-no-go}
Let an exact constant-$\ell$ solution be $C^{2}$ in a neighbourhood of
$[R_{1},R_{2}]\times\{0\}$, and set $u(r):=v(r,0)$. Assume that
\begin{enumerate}
\item
the solution is reflection symmetric with respect to the equatorial plane, and $0<u(r)<1$ for all $r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]$;
\item
the density is nonnegative along the trace, $\rho(r,0)\geqslant 0$ for all $r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]$;
\item
the reflection surface is vertically localized in the invariant sense
$N(N\psi)\leqslant0$, equivalently $v_{zz}(r,0)\leqslant0$, for all
$r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]$;
\item
the equatorial trace is $\varepsilon$-flat for some $0\leqslant\varepsilon<1$.
\end{enumerate}
Let us define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vmin-approx-flat}
\underline v:=\min_{R_{1}\leqslant r\leqslant R_{2}}u(r)>0.
\end{equation}
Then, the logarithmic radial span $\Lambda=\log\!\left(R_{2}/R_{1}\right)$ satisfies the following bounds.
\begin{itemize}
\item
If $\ellzero\leqslant 0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-flat-bound-nonpositive-l}
\Lambda\leqslant2\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon
=\log\!\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\right).
\end{equation}
\item
If $\ellzero>0$, then for every $\alpha\in(0,1-\varepsilon)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-flat-bound-positive-l}
\Lambda
\leqslant\mathcal B_{+}(\varepsilon,\alpha;\underline v):=\frac{\varepsilon(2+\alpha)}{(1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\log\!\left[\frac{2}{(1+\underline v^{2})\alpha}\right].
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Thus, an annulus whose logarithmic radial span exceeds the corresponding
bound cannot support a solution satisfying all four hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let us first recall that Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-equatorial-log-identity} gives the density bound \eqref{eq:y-density-domain}. We first show that the coefficient $A=2+y(1-u^{2})$ is strictly positive throughout $[R_{1},R_{2}]$. If $\ellzero=0$, then $y=0$ and hence, $A=2$. If $\ellzero>0$, then
$y>0$, so $A>2$. It remains to consider $\ellzero<0$. In this case, the
lower density bound implies $A\geqslant 0$. Suppose that $A=0$ at some point.
Then necessarily $y=-2/(1-u^{2})$. At that point, the term
$A(Z+q)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-equatorial-log-identity} vanishes, and
the remaining terms reduce to $(s+1)\left[(1+u^{2})s-(3-u^{2})\right]=0$. The two possible values are $s=-1$, and $s=(3-u^{2})/(1+u^{2})>1$, both of which contradict $|s|\leqslant\varepsilon<1$. Therefore, $A>0$ throughout the trace, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:master-approx-flat-inequality} applies everywhere.
\begin{itemize}
\item
\textbf{Case $\ellzero\leqslant0$}: Subtract
$A(1-s^2)$ from the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:master-approx-flat-inequality}. Exact factorization gives
\begin{align}
&2(1-y)-2(1-yu^2)s^2-2y(1-u^2)s-A(1-s^2)
\nonumber\\
&\hspace{2cm}=-y(1+s)
\left[3-u^2-(1+u^2)s\right].
\label{eq:sharp-negative-l-factorization}
\end{align}
Here $y\leqslant0$, $1+s>0$, and the final factor is strictly positive
because $0<u<1$ and $s<1$. Therefore $q\geqslant1-s^2$. Dividing by
$1-s^2$ and integrating gives
$$
\Lambda\leqslant
\operatorname{artanh}s(\Lambda)-\operatorname{artanh}s(0)
\leqslant2\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon,
$$
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-nonpositive-l}.
\item
\textbf{Case $\ellzero>0$}: In this case, $y>0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-derivative-identities}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-growth-approx-flat}
\frac{d\log y}{dt}=1-s\geqslant 1-\varepsilon>0,
\end{equation}
so $y$ is strictly increasing with $t$. The upper density bound also
implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-upper-vmin}
0<y(r)\leqslant\frac{2}{1+u(r)^{2}}\leqslant Y_{\max}:=\frac{2}{1+\underline v^{2}}<2.
\end{equation}
Let us fix $\alpha\in(0,1-\varepsilon)$. At every point where
$y\leqslant\alpha$, we have $0<1-yu^{2}\leqslant1$ and $A\leqslant2+\alpha$. Using $|s|\leqslant\varepsilon$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:master-approx-flat-inequality}, its right-hand side is bounded below by $2(1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)$. Therefore, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-lower-small-y}
q\geqslant c_{\alpha}:=\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)}{2+\alpha}>0
\end{equation}
whenever $y\leqslant\alpha$. Because $y$ is strictly increasing, the set on which $y\leqslant\alpha$ is, if nonempty, an initial portion of the logarithmic radial interval. Integrating $q=ds/dt\geqslant c_{\alpha}$ over this portion and using
$-\varepsilon\leqslant s\leqslant\varepsilon$ shows that its length is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-y-length-bound}
\frac{2\varepsilon}{c_{\alpha}}=\frac{\varepsilon(2+\alpha)}
{(1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
This estimate also applies if $y$ remains below $\alpha$ throughout the
entire interval. Once $y\geqslant\alpha$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:y-growth-approx-flat} shows that $\log y$ increases at least at rate $1-\varepsilon$. Since $y\leqslant Y_{\max}$, the remaining logarithmic radial length is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large-y-length-bound}
\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\log\!\left(\frac{Y_{\max}}{\alpha}\right)=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\log\!\left[\frac{2}{(1+\underline v^{2})\alpha}\right].
\end{equation}
Adding the bounds in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:small-y-length-bound} and \eqref{eq:large-y-length-bound} yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-positive-l}.
\end{itemize}
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharpness of the nonpositive-$\ell$ span bound]
\label{cor:sharp-nonpositive-l-span}
For every $0<\varepsilon<1$, the first bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-approx-flat-no-go} is sharp over the class
$\ellzero\leqslant0$. Equality is attained by the rigid branch
$\ellzero=0$ with
\begin{equation}
u(t)=a\cosh(t-t_0),\qquad
s(t)=\tanh(t-t_0),
\label{eq:sharp-span-extremal}
\end{equation}
restricted to
$t-t_0\in[-\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon,
\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon]$ and with $a>0$ sufficiently small.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $\ellzero=0$, $\mathcal F=2\eta$. The trace in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sharp-span-extremal} gives
$$
\eta=ru=\frac a2\left(\frac{r^2}{r_0}+r_0\right),
$$
so $\mathcal F=c_2r^2+c_0$ is $\mathcal L$-harmonic and independent of
$z$. Thus $v_{zz}=0$, and the exact VFE, density, and vertical-localisation
hypotheses hold on every compact subinterval on which $0<u<1$ and
$\nabla\eta\neq0$. Moreover $s$ runs from $-\varepsilon$ to
$+\varepsilon$ in logarithmic time
$2\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon$. The two inequalities used in the
proof are equalities, establishing sharpness.
\end{proof}

The positive-$\ell$ estimate in Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-approx-flat-no-go} holds for every $\alpha\in(0,1-\varepsilon)$. We now optimize over this auxiliary parameter and examine the limit in which approximate flatness approaches exact flatness.

\begin{corollary}[Optimized positive-$\ell$ span bound and exact-flat limit]
\label{cor:optimized-approx-flat-span}
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, let us define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K-epsilon}
K_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon(3-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-optimal}
\alpha_{\varepsilon}:=1-\varepsilon+\frac{K_{\varepsilon}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{
K_{\varepsilon}\left[K_{\varepsilon}+4(1-\varepsilon)\right]}.
\end{equation}
Then, $0<\alpha_{\varepsilon}<1-\varepsilon$, and $\alpha_{\varepsilon}$ is the unique value of $\alpha$ in $(0,1-\varepsilon)$ that minimizes the function
$\mathcal B_{+}(\varepsilon,\alpha;\underline v)$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-positive-l}. Consequently, for $\ellzero>0$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimized-positive-l-ratio}
\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\leqslant\exp\!\left[\mathcal B_{+}(\varepsilon,\alpha_{\varepsilon};\underline v)
\right].
\end{equation}
In the limit $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$, the optimized bound becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimized-exact-flat-limit}
\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\leqslant\frac{2}{1+\underline v^{2}}.
\end{equation}
For an exactly flat trace, $\underline v=v_{0}$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimized-exact-flat-limit} agrees with the exact-flat span bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:flat-trace-span-ratio}. For fixed $\underline v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimized-small-epsilon-expansion}
\inf_{0<\alpha<1-\varepsilon}\mathcal B_{+}(\varepsilon,\alpha;\underline v)
=\log\!\left(\frac{2}{1+\underline v^{2}}\right)+2\sqrt{3\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon)
\end{equation}
as $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $\underline v$, let us consider the function
$\mathcal B_{+}(\varepsilon,\alpha;\underline v)$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-positive-l}. It diverges as $\alpha\downarrow 0$ and as $\alpha\uparrow 1-\varepsilon$. Its derivative with respect to $\alpha$ vanishes precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-stationary-equation}
(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)^{2}=K_{\varepsilon}\alpha.
\end{equation}
The function $\alpha\longmapsto(1-\varepsilon-\alpha)^{2}/\alpha$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1-\varepsilon)$, from $+\infty$ to $0$. Hence, Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha-stationary-equation} has a unique solution in
this interval. Solving the quadratic equation and selecting that root
gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha-optimal}. Since $\mathcal B_{+}$ diverges at both endpoints, this critical point is the unique global minimizer. Equation~\eqref{eq:optimized-positive-l-ratio} then follows directly from
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-approx-flat-no-go}. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
$K_{\varepsilon}=3\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})$, and $\alpha_{\varepsilon}=
1-\sqrt{3\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon)$. Thus, $\alpha_{\varepsilon}\to 1$, and substitution into the optimized bound gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimized-exact-flat-limit}. For the small-$\varepsilon$ expansion, the rational term in
$\mathcal B_{+}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\varepsilon(2+\alpha_{\varepsilon})}{(1+\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon-\alpha_{\varepsilon})}=\sqrt{3\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon),
\end{equation}
while $-\log\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{3\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon)$. Since $(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}=1+O(\varepsilon)$, combining these
expansions yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimized-small-epsilon-expansion}.
\end{proof}

As an immediate consequence, the preceding bounds also exclude a semi-infinite approximately flat trace. Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-approx-flat-no-go} hold on every finite
subinterval $[R_{1},R_{2}]$ of $[R_{1},\infty)$. If $\ellzero\leqslant 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-nonpositive-l} gives
\begin{equation}
\log\!\left(\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\right)
\leqslant2\operatorname{artanh}\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
which is incompatible with $R_{2}\to\infty$. For $\ellzero>0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimized-positive-l-ratio} likewise
provides a finite upper bound on $R_{2}/R_{1}$ when
$0<\varepsilon<1$. Indeed, since $\underline v^{2}\geqslant 0$, the right-hand side is bounded above by the same expression evaluated at $\underline v=0$. At $\varepsilon=0$, the positive-$\ellzero$ exact-flat bound
Eq.~\eqref{eq:flat-trace-span-ratio} gives the finite ratio directly.
Hence, an $\varepsilon$-flat trace with $0\leqslant\varepsilon<1$ cannot persist on $[R_{1},\infty)$ under the stated density and vertical-localization conditions. More generally, any proposed approximately flat interval is excluded whenever its logarithmic radial span exceeds the bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:approx-flat-bound-nonpositive-l} for $\ellzero\leqslant 0$, or its radial ratio exceeds the optimized bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimized-positive-l-ratio} for $\ellzero>0$.


\section{Asymptotic flatness and the constant-\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} logarithmic branch}
\label{sec:asymptotic-obstruction}

The preceding analysis constrains constant-$\ell$ configurations on finite radial domains. We now ask whether such a branch can extend to an asymptotically flat isolated end. We first test the logarithmic retained-order solution of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} against the falloff required in an asymptotically nonrotating frame. We then examine the range of validity of the approximation that produces this logarithmic behaviour and derive the corresponding obstruction directly from the exact constant-$\ell$ metric. This distinction separates a failure of the retained-order asymptotic ansatz from the stronger constraints imposed by the exact solution. Before carrying out the asymptotic test, it is important to distinguish the angular velocity of the dust from that of the ZAMO congruence. Equation~(4.7) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} gives the coordinate angular velocity
$\Omega=d\phi/dt$ of the dust, whereas the angular velocity of the ZAMO
congruence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zamo-shift-definition}
\chi:=-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}.
\end{equation}
Up to the conventional sign, $-\chi$ is the azimuthal component of the
shift vector in coordinates adapted to stationarity and axisymmetry. Thus, Eq.~(4.7) is not, by itself, a formula for the metric shift. The same logarithmic dependence appears in the shift only after Eq.~(4.7) is combined with the metric components in Eq.~(4.8). Under the constant-field and constant-$\ell$ approximation adopted in Sect.~4 of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, and after absorbing the additive integration constant into a positive scale $r_{0}$, we find
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-omega}
\Omega^{(1)}(r)=-\frac{\ellzero}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right),
\end{equation}
together with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-metric-components}
g_{tt}^{(1)}=-1,\qquad
g_{t\phi}^{(1)}=\frac{\ellzero}{2}r^{2}\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)+v_{0}r,\qquad
g_{\phi\phi}^{(1)}=r^{2}.
\end{equation}
The superscript $(1)$ indicates that these expressions belong to the
retained-order approximation. They are not exact large-radius
asymptotic expansions. Equations~\eqref{eq:zamo-shift-definition} and
\eqref{eq:logarithmic-metric-components} then give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-zamo-shift}
\chi^{(1)}(r)=-\frac{\ellzero}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)-\frac{v_{0}}{r}.
\end{equation}
The first asymptotic obstruction derived below depends only on the
growth order of these terms and is therefore insensitive to a possible
factor-of-two convention for the mixed term in the line element.

\subsection{Asymptotic-flatness test of the retained-order branch}

To test the retained-order branch against asymptotic flatness, we first
translate the standard falloff conditions in asymptotically Cartesian
coordinates into the azimuthal metric components relevant to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:logarithmic-metric-components}. Let $\mathcal R:=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}$, and $\varrho:=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$, where $(t,x,y,z)$ are asymptotically Cartesian coordinates on the
spatial end. We assume the weak asymptotic falloff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-cartesian-falloff}
g_{tt}=-1+O(\mathcal R^{-1}),\qquad
g_{ti}=O(\mathcal R^{-1}),\qquad
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(\mathcal R^{-1}),
\end{equation}
with $i,j\in\{x,y,z\}$, in an asymptotically nonrotating frame. The
usual derivative falloffs may also be imposed, but they are not needed
for the argument below. Conditions of this form are standard in the
asymptotically flat isolated-system framework (see, for example,
Refs.~\cite{Regge1974AnnPhys,BeigSimon1980GRG,Beig1987AnnPhys}). No use
will be made of the exterior field equations or of the stronger stationary-vacuum multipole expansion.

\begin{lemma}[Azimuthal falloff implied by asymptotic flatness]
\label{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff}
Let us assume Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-cartesian-falloff}, and choose the asymptotic
rotation axis to be the $z$-axis. Let $k=\partial_t$ and $m=\partial_\phi=-y\partial_x+x\partial_y$ denote the stationary and axial coordinate vector fields. Along the equatorial end $z=0$, where $\mathcal R=\varrho$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-cylindrical-components}
g_{t\phi}=g(k,m)=O(1),\qquad
g_{\phi\phi}=g(m,m)=\varrho^{2}+O(\varrho)
\end{equation}
as $\varrho\to\infty$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:af-shift-falloff}
\chi=-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}=O(\varrho^{-2}).
\end{equation}
The same estimates hold on any fixed cone away from the rotation axis
for which $\varrho/\mathcal R$ is bounded below by a positive constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From $m=-y\partial_{x}+x\partial_{y}$, we obtain $g_{t\phi}=g(k,m)=
-y\,g_{tx}+x\,g_{ty}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:af-cartesian-falloff} gives
$g_{tx},g_{ty}=O(\mathcal R^{-1})$, while $|x|+|y|=O(\varrho)$. Hence, $
g_{t\phi}=O\!\left(\varrho/\mathcal R\right)$. Along the equatorial end, where $\mathcal R=\varrho$, this reduces to $g_{t\phi}=O(1)$. For the azimuthal component, the Euclidean norm of the axial vector is $\delta_{ij}m^{i}m^{j}=
x^{2}+y^{2}=\varrho^{2}$. Using
$g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(\mathcal R^{-1})$, we therefore find
\begin{equation}
g_{\phi\phi}=g_{ij}m^{i}m^{j}=\delta_{ij}m^{i}m^{j}+(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})m^{i}m^{j}=\varrho^{2}+O\!\left(\frac{\varrho^{2}}{\mathcal R}\right).
\end{equation}
On the equatorial end, $\mathcal R=\varrho$, and hence, $g_{\phi\phi}=
\varrho^{2}+O(\varrho)=\varrho^{2}\left[1+O(\varrho^{-1})\right]$. Combining this estimate with $g_{t\phi}=O(1)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\chi=-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}=O(\varrho^{-2}),
\end{equation}
which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-shift-falloff}. Finally, on any fixed cone for which $\varrho/\mathcal R$ is bounded below by a positive constant,
$\varrho$ and $\mathcal R$ have the same asymptotic order. The preceding
estimates therefore give $g_{t\phi}=O(1)$, $g_{\phi\phi}=\varrho^{2}+O(\varrho)$, and $\chi=O(\varrho^{-2})$ there as well.
\end{proof}

The preceding lemma expresses the asymptotic-flatness conditions in
terms of the ordinary cylindrical distance $\varrho$. The constant-$\ell$ metric, however, is written in terms of the usual orbit-area coordinate $r$. These two radial variables agree to leading order on an asymptotically flat end. Indeed, using the exact identity $g_{tt}g_{\phi\phi}-g_{t\phi}^{2}=-r^{2}$ together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-cartesian-falloff} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} gives, along the equatorial end,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics}
r^{2}=\varrho^{2}+O(\varrho),\qquad
\frac{r}{\varrho}=1+O(\varrho^{-1})
\end{equation}
as $\varrho\to\infty$. Thus, the asymptotic estimates of Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} may equivalently be expressed in terms of $r$.



\begin{proposition}[Asymptotic obstruction for the retained-order logarithmic branch]\label{prop:asymptotic-obstruction}
Let a stationary, axisymmetric metric possess an asymptotically flat
end satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-cartesian-falloff} in coordinates
adapted to $k=\partial_t$ and $m=\partial_\phi$. Suppose that, along the
equatorial part of that end, the retained-order logarithmic branch of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:logarithmic-omega}--\eqref{eq:logarithmic-metric-components} gives the leading asymptotic behaviour of the metric in the form
\begin{align}
g_{t\phi}(r,0)&=\frac{\ellzero}{2}r^{2}\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)
+v_{0}r+\mathcal E_{t\phi}(r),\label{eq:asymptotic-log-ansatz}\\
g_{\phi\phi}(r,0)&=r^{2}\bigl[1+\mathcal E_{\phi}(r)\bigr],\label{eq:asymptotic-gphiphi-ansatz}
\end{align}
where $r$ is the usual orbit-area radius, $r_{0}>0$, $v_{0}$ is
finite, $\ellzero$ is constant, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymptotic-remainders}
\mathcal E_{t\phi}(r)=o\!\left(r^{2}\log r\right),\qquad
\mathcal E_{\phi}(r)=o(1)
\end{equation}
for $r\to\infty$. Then, asymptotic flatness requires $\ellzero=0$. Consequently, a nonzero constant $\ellzero$ and the logarithmically
growing retained-order branch obtained from Eqs.~(4.7)--(4.8) of
Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} cannot describe an asymptotically flat
isolated end.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $r_{0}>0$ is fixed, $\log(r/r_{0})/\log r\longrightarrow 1$ as
$r\to\infty$. Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-log-ansatz} by
$r^{2}\log r$ gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{g_{t\phi}(r,0)}{r^{2}\log r}=\frac{\ellzero}{2}
\frac{\log(r/r_{0})}{\log r}+\frac{v_{0}}{r\log r}+\frac{\mathcal E_{t\phi}(r)}{r^{2}\log r}.
\end{equation}
The second term tends to zero because $v_{0}$ is finite, while the
third tends to zero by Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-remainders}. Therefore,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gtphi-log-limit}
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{g_{t\phi}(r,0)}{r^{2}\log r}=\frac{\ellzero}{2}.
\end{equation}
If $\ellzero\neq 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gtphi-log-limit} shows that $g_{t\phi}$ grows in magnitude as $r^{2}\log r$. On the other hand, Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} requires $g_{t\phi}=O(1)$ in terms of $\varrho$, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics} gives $r/\varrho\to 1$. Hence, the same $O(1)$ bound holds when the end is parametrized by $r$. This contradiction excludes $\ellzero\neq 0$. The same obstruction can be expressed in terms of the ZAMO angular velocity. Equations~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-log-ansatz} and \eqref{eq:asymptotic-gphiphi-ansatz} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chi-log-limit}
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\chi(r,0)}{\log r}=-\frac{\ellzero}{2},
\end{equation}
whereas Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff}, together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics}, requires $\chi=O(r^{-2})$. Thus, asymptotic flatness necessarily requires $\ellzero=0$.
\end{proof}

The preceding proposition tests the logarithmic branch through the
asymptotic behaviour of the metric. The same logarithmic growth produces
an independent obstruction at the level of the dust congruence itself.
Indeed, a circular trajectory whose coordinate angular velocity grows
logarithmically cannot remain timelike at arbitrarily large radius on an
asymptotically flat end.

\begin{corollary}[No timelike circular dust motion with logarithmic angular velocity]\label{cor:logarithmic-dust-spacelike}
Under the asymptotic-flatness hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff}, let us consider a circular dust congruence extending to arbitrarily large equatorial values of the orbit-area radius $r$. Suppose that its coordinate angular velocity satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logarithmic-dust-omega-asymptotic}
\Omega(r)=-\frac{\ellzero}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)+o(\log r)
\end{equation}
with $\ellzero\neq 0$ and $r_{0}>0$. Then, the circular tangent direction becomes spacelike for all sufficiently large $r$. Consequently, such a congruence cannot remain a timelike dust flow on an asymptotically flat end.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A circular trajectory with coordinate angular velocity $\Omega$ has
tangent direction $K:=k+\Omega m$, where $k=\partial_t$ and $m=\partial_\phi$. Such a direction can be normalized to a timelike four-velocity only where $g(K,K)<0$. Its squared norm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-generator-norm-prop3}
g(K,K)=g(k,k)+2\Omega\,g(k,m)+\Omega^{2}g(m,m).
\end{equation}
Along the asymptotically flat equatorial end, Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} gives $g(k,k)=-1+O(\varrho^{-1})$, $g(k,m)=O(1)$, and $g(m,m)=\varrho^{2}+O(\varrho)$. Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics}, these estimates may equivalently be written in terms of the radius $r$ as $g(k,k)=-1+O(r^{-1})$, $g(k,m)=O(1)$, and $g(m,m)=r^{2}\bigl[1+o(1)\bigr]$. Equation~\eqref{eq:logarithmic-dust-omega-asymptotic} with $\ellzero\neq 0$ implies $|\Omega(r)|\,r\longrightarrow\infty$. Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:circular-generator-norm-prop3} by the positive quantity $\Omega^{2}r^{2}$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{g(K,K)}{\Omega^{2}r^{2}}=\frac{g(k,k)}{\Omega^{2}r^{2}}+
\frac{2g(k,m)}{\Omega r^{2}}+\frac{g(m,m)}{r^{2}}\longrightarrow 1.
\end{equation}
Hence, $g(K,K)>0$ for all sufficiently large $r$. The circular tangent direction is therefore eventually spacelike and cannot be normalized to a timelike dust four-velocity.
\end{proof}

The literal retained-order metric is even more restrictive. If the
remainder terms in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-log-ansatz} and
\eqref{eq:asymptotic-gphiphi-ansatz} are set to zero, i.e. $\mathcal E_{t\phi}=0$, $\mathcal E_{\phi}=0$, Proposition~\ref{prop:asymptotic-obstruction} already excludes every $\ellzero\neq 0$. If instead $\ellzero=0$ while a nonzero constant $v_{0}$ is retained all the way to spatial infinity, then $g_{t\phi}^{(1)}=v_{0}r$. This linear growth is likewise incompatible with the asymptotic-flatness condition $g_{t\phi}=O(1)$. Thus, an exactly flat nonzero velocity profile cannot extend to infinity within the literal retained-order metric even when $\ellzero=0$. This stronger observation is not needed for
Proposition~\ref{prop:asymptotic-obstruction}, whose purpose is to
isolate the obstruction produced specifically by the logarithmic term
associated with a nonzero constant $\ellzero$. One might ask whether this logarithmic growth can instead be removed by a change of angular coordinates. Changing the reference scale $r_{0}$ only shifts $\log(r/r_{0})$ by an additive constant and therefore leaves its radial growth unchanged. Likewise, a rigid angular transformation $\widetilde\phi=\phi-\omega_{0}t$ can change the angular shift only by a constant. It cannot remove a term proportional to $\log r$. Moreover, the corresponding stationary generator is $k_{\omega_{0}}=k+\omega_{0}m$. Using the asymptotic estimates established above gives
\begin{equation}
g(k_{\omega_{0}},k_{\omega_{0}})=\omega_{0}^{2}r^{2}+O(r)-1
\end{equation}
as $r\to\infty$. For every $\omega_{0}\neq0$, this generator is therefore spacelike at sufficiently large radius. A rigidly rotating coordinate system cannot serve as an asymptotically inertial frame on the isolated end. Nor can the logarithmic dependence be removed by allowing the angular rotation to depend on radius. A transformation of the form $\widetilde\phi=\phi-F(r)t$ implies
\begin{equation}
d\phi=d\widetilde\phi+F(r)\,dt+tF'(r)\,dr.
\end{equation}
Whenever $F'(r)\neq 0$, substitution into the metric introduces explicit
dependence on $t$, so the transformed coordinates are no longer adapted
to stationarity. The logarithmic term therefore cannot be eliminated
within the class of stationary, asymptotically inertial coordinate
systems relevant to an asymptotically flat isolated end.

\subsection{Nonuniformity of the retained-order expansion and the exact obstruction}

The preceding proposition is necessarily conditional because it tests a
retained-order metric. It assumes that the displayed logarithmic term
continues to give the leading asymptotic behaviour, with corrections
smaller than $r^{2}\log r$. The approximation used to derive
Eq.~(4.7) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC}, however, does not provide a
uniform remainder estimate of this kind. Indeed, Eq.~(4.6) of Ref.~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} uses the linearization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-linearization-asymptotic-section}
H=-e^{\ellzero\eta}=-e^{\ellzero v_{0}r}\simeq
-\bigl(1+\ellzero v_{0}r\bigr),
\end{equation}
whose dimensionless expansion parameter is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonuniform-expansion-parameter}
\varepsilon(r):=\ellzero\eta=\ellzero v_{0}r.
\end{equation}
For fixed $\ellzero v_{0}\neq 0$, $|\varepsilon(r)|\longrightarrow\infty$
as $r\to\infty$. The linearization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H-linearization-asymptotic-section} is therefore not uniform on an unbounded exterior end. The same loss of control is visible directly in the corresponding asymptotically orthonormal azimuthal component
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthonormal-mixed-growth}
\frac{g_{t\phi}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{g_{\phi\phi}^{(1)}}}=\frac{\ellzero}{2}
r\log\!\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)+v_{0},
\end{equation}
which cannot remain a small perturbation at arbitrarily large radius
when $\ellzero\neq 0$. This leads to two possibilities. If the retained-order logarithmic term continues to dominate on the asymptotic end, then
Proposition~\ref{prop:asymptotic-obstruction} excludes asymptotic
flatness. If higher-order terms instead become comparable and modify
the leading behaviour, then Eq.~(4.7) no longer determines the
asymptotic exterior. In neither case does the retained-order logarithmic
formula establish an asymptotically flat constant-$\ell$ exterior. The exact constant-$\ell$ relations allow this issue to be settled without relying on the large-radius validity of the retained-order expansion. In particular, the exact metric itself excludes an asymptotically flat end whenever the equatorial speed approaches a nonzero constant.

\begin{corollary}[Exact asymptotic obstruction for a nonzero limiting speed]
\label{cor:exact-asymptotic-obstruction}
Consider an exact constant-$\ell$ rotating-dust solution on an unbounded equatorial end, with $\ell(\eta)\equiv\ellzero\neq 0$. Suppose that, in terms of the orbit-area radius $r$, $v(r,0)\longrightarrow v_{\infty}\in(0,1)$ as $r\to\infty$. Then the exact metric cannot be asymptotically flat on that end.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-metric-identities}, $g_{\phi\phi}=(r^{2}-\eta^{2})/(-H)$. For a constant-$\ell$ branch, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-H} gives $H=-C e^{\ellzero\eta}$ with $C>0$. Using $\eta=rv$, we therefore obtain the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-gphiphi-ratio}
\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{r^{2}}=\frac{1-v^{2}}{C e^{\ellzero rv}}.
\end{equation}
By hypothesis, $v(r,0)\longrightarrow v_{\infty}\in(0,1)$, and hence, $rv(r,0)\longrightarrow+\infty$, and $1-v(r,0)^{2}\longrightarrow 1-v_{\infty}^{2}>0$. It follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-gphiphi-ratio} that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-gphiphi-limits}
\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{r^{2}}\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
0, & \ellzero>0,\\[2mm]
+\infty, & \ellzero<0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
If the end were asymptotically flat, Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} would give $g_{\phi\phi}/\varrho^{2}\longrightarrow 1$. Moreover, Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics} gives $r/\varrho\longrightarrow 1$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{r^{2}}=\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{\varrho^{2}}
\frac{\varrho^{2}}{r^{2}}\longrightarrow 1,
\end{equation}
which contradicts both alternatives in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-gphiphi-limits}. Therefore, the exact metric cannot be asymptotically flat on the assumed end.
\end{proof}

The preceding corollary excludes a nonzero limiting equatorial speed.
The same exact metric identity yields a stronger restriction. If a
nonzero constant-$\ell$ branch is to remain asymptotically flat while
staying uniformly subluminal, then its equatorial speed must decay at
least as fast as the inverse radius.

\begin{corollary}[Inverse-radius decay required by asymptotic flatness]
\label{cor:exact-asymptotic-decay}
Let an exact constant-$\ell$ branch with $\ellzero\neq 0$ extend along an
asymptotically flat equatorial end, with $r$ denoting the orbit-area radius. Assume that the equatorial speed is uniformly subluminal for sufficiently large $r$. More precisely, suppose that there exist $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $R_{0}>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-subluminality-asymptotic}
0<v(r,0)\leqslant 1-\delta
\end{equation}
for all $r\geqslant R_{0}$. Then,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-inverse-radius-decay}
r\,v(r,0)=O(1),\qquad
v(r,0)=O(r^{-1})
\end{equation}
as $r\to\infty$. In particular, an asymptotically flat end of the same nonzero
constant-$\ell$ branch can neither approach a positive limiting speed
nor exhibit a positive decay slower than inverse radius.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Asymptotic flatness and Lemma~\ref{lem:af-azimuthal-falloff} give $g_{\phi\phi}/\varrho^{2}\longrightarrow 1$. Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:canonical-cylindrical-asymptotics}, which gives
$r/\varrho\to 1$, this implies $g_{\phi\phi}/r^{2}\longrightarrow 1$. Taking the logarithm of the exact identity Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-gphiphi-ratio} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-exact-asymptotic-identity}
\ellzero r v=\log(1-v^{2})-\log C-\log\!\left(\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{r^{2}}\right).
\end{equation}
The uniform subluminality condition
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-subluminality-asymptotic} implies $\delta(2-\delta)\leqslant
1-v^{2}<1$, so $\log(1-v^{2})$ remains bounded. Moreover, $\log\!\left(g_{\phi\phi}/r^{2}\right)\longrightarrow 0$, while $\log C$ is constant. The right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:log-exact-asymptotic-identity} is therefore bounded, and hence, $\ellzero r v=O(1)$. Since $\ellzero\neq 0$ is constant, $rv=O(1)$, which immediately gives $v=O(r^{-1})$ and proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-inverse-radius-decay}.
\end{proof}

The boundedness conclusion can be sharpened to an exact locking law. This
also exposes the role of the integration constant $C$, which cannot be
discarded before the asymptotic normalization is imposed.

\begin{theorem}[Exact asymptotic locking]
\label{thm:exact-asymptotic-locking}
Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-asymptotic-decay}, the
limit
\begin{equation}
\eta(r,0)=r v(r,0)\longrightarrow
q:=-\frac{\log C}{\ellzero}
\label{eq:asymptotic-locking-q}
\end{equation}
exists. If the positive-speed dust branch itself reaches the end, then
$q>0$ and timelikeness forces $\Omega(q)=0$. If, in
addition,
$$
\frac{g_{\phi\phi}}{r^2}=1+O(r^{-2}),
$$
then
\begin{equation}
\eta=q+O(r^{-2}),\qquad
v=\frac qr+O(r^{-3}),\qquad
H=-1+O(r^{-2}),\qquad
\Omega=O(r^{-2}).
\label{eq:asymptotic-locking-orders}
\end{equation}
In particular, the normalization $C=1$ is incompatible with a positive
constant-$\ellzero\neq0$ dust end.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-asymptotic-decay} makes $\eta$ bounded and hence
$v=\eta/r\to0$. Taking the limit of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:log-exact-asymptotic-identity} gives
$\ellzero\eta\to-\log C$, proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-locking-q}. Positivity gives $q\geqslant0$. If
$q=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-l-Omega} has logarithmic divergence, and the
norm of $k+\Omega m$ is eventually positive by the same comparison used in
Corollary~\ref{cor:logarithmic-dust-spacelike}. Hence a positive timelike
dust end requires $q>0$. At a positive limiting $q$, $\Omega$ is smooth.
If $\Omega(q)\neq0$, asymptotic flatness makes the positive term
$\Omega^2g_{\phi\phi}$ in
$H=g(k+\Omega m,k+\Omega m)$ grow quadratically, while the stationary and
mixed terms remain lower order. This contradicts both timelikeness and
$H\to-Ce^{\ellzero q}=-1$. Therefore $\Omega(q)=0$. In particular, one has
$\Omega^2g_{\phi\phi}\to0$ and $\Omega g_{t\phi}\to0$.

Under the stronger displayed falloff, the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:log-exact-asymptotic-identity} differs from $-\log C$ by
$O(v^2)+O(r^{-2})=O(r^{-2})$. This yields the first two estimates in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-locking-orders}. Expanding
$H=-Ce^{\ellzero\eta}$ about $q$, using
$Ce^{\ellzero q}=1$, gives the third. Finally,
$\Omega(q)=0$ and smoothness of $\Omega$ at $q>0$ give the fourth. For
$C=1$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-locking-q} gives $q=0$, which was just
excluded.
\end{proof}

These asymptotic results do not exclude a dust region of finite extent
or a smooth transition away from the constant-$\ell$ branch before the
asymptotic region is reached. The finite-boundary alternative requires
a separate junction analysis, which is developed next.

\section{Finite boundaries and vacuum matching}
\label{sec:vacuum-matching}

The asymptotic analysis leaves open the possibility that the constant-$\ell$ dust region terminates at finite radius and is joined to a different vacuum exterior. Reference~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} suggests that the radius at which the dust density vanishes may be interpreted as a characteristic galactic scale. Although $\rho=0$ provides a natural candidate location for a material boundary, it is not by itself a junction condition. A symmetry-preserving matching must instead satisfy the Darmois conditions, namely continuity of the first and second fundamental forms of the matching hypersurface
\cite{Darmois1927,Israel1966NCB,MarsSenovilla1993CQG}. If the induced metric is continuous while the second fundamental form has a jump, the matching carries an Israel surface stress tensor. We first identify the regular components of the zero-density set that can serve as candidate matching hypersurfaces. We then reduce the Darmois--Israel data to the stationary, axisymmetric variables used throughout this work and classify the resulting local matching
alternatives. Finally, we return to the finite density cutoffs obtained earlier and distinguish the local junction conditions from the separate problem of
constructing a globally regular, asymptotically flat vacuum exterior.

This local/global distinction has substantial precedent. Zingg, Aste, and
Trautmann identified the overdetermination produced by prescribing both
metric and normal data for a general non-rigid dust interior
\cite{ZinggAsteTrautmann2007ASTP}. On the other hand, controlled Dirichlet
existence and uniqueness results are available for restricted stationary
axisymmetric vacuum boundary problems
\cite{SchaudtPfister1996PRL,Schaudt1998CMP,Klenk1999ANZIAM}, and Marvan
constructed a one-parameter Papapetrou-vacuum matching for every rigid van
Stockum dust solution whose boundary exists \cite{Marvan2023JGP}. These
results are complementary: Dirichlet solvability does not realize arbitrary
overdetermined Darmois data, while the rigid construction does not provide a
constant-$\ellzero\neq0$ exterior theorem.


\subsection{Zero-density sets and candidate matching surfaces}

We begin by identifying which parts of the zero-density set can define a
regular symmetry-preserving boundary. To this purpose, we recall the block form
Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-block-metric}. The exact identity $\det G=-r^{2}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-metric-identities} shows that, away from the rotation axis, the Killing block $G$ has Lorentzian signature. At a regular point with $r>0$ and $0<v<1$, the density decomposition Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-sign-decomposition} together with the factorization Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-factorization} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-density-decomposition}
\{\rho=0\}=\mathcal C\cup\mathcal Z_{-}\cup\mathcal Z_{+},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C:=\bigl\{(r,z):\nabla_{rz}\eta=0\bigr\},\quad
\mathcal Z_{-}:=\left\{(r,z):r\ell=-\frac{2v}{1-v^{2}}\right\},\quad
\mathcal Z_{+}:=\left\{(r,z):r\ell=\frac{2v}{1+v^{2}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The critical set $\mathcal C$ may intersect either algebraic branch,
whereas $\mathcal Z_{-}\cap\mathcal Z_{+}=\varnothing$ for $0<v<1$. A point of the meridional zero set does not by itself define a matching surface. A regular symmetry-preserving candidate boundary is generated instead by a smooth embedded curve $\gamma\subset\{\rho=0\}$ in the meridional plane. Acting on $\gamma$ with the stationary and axial symmetries produces the three-dimensional
hypersurface
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetry-generated-boundary}
\Sigma=\mathbb R_{t}\times S^{1}_{\phi}\times\gamma.
\end{equation}
As long as $\Sigma$ lies in a regular region with $r>0$, it is timelike. Indeed, the Killing block has Lorentzian signature because $\det G=-r^{2}<0$, while the tangent direction along $\gamma$ has positive norm in the meridional metric $e^{\mu}(dr^{2}+dz^{2})$. A nonsmooth component of the zero set, a
component passing through a metric singularity, or the degenerate orbit
$r=0$ does not define a standard timelike Darmois hypersurface and
requires separate treatment. Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-density-decomposition} therefore provides an exhaustive classification of the zero-density set at regular points, but not yet a classification of admissible matchings. In particular, the scalar conditions defining $\mathcal Z_{-}$ and $\mathcal Z_{+}$
do not determine the full first and second fundamental forms of $\Sigma$. The required boundary data are derived in the next subsection.

\subsection{Symmetry-reduced Darmois--Israel data and local classification}

Having identified the regular zero-density sets that can provide candidate boundaries, we now compute the geometric data required to test whether such a boundary can be matched to a vacuum exterior. Let $\gamma$ be a smooth meridional curve generating the timelike hypersurface $\Sigma$ introduced in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:symmetry-generated-boundary}. We parametrize $\gamma$ by
arclength $\lambda$ with respect to the auxiliary flat meridional metric $\delta=dr^{2}+dz^{2}$, so that $\gamma(\lambda)=\bigl(r(\lambda),z(\lambda)\bigr)$ and $\dot r^{2}+\dot z^{2}=1$. The corresponding unit tangent and a chosen unit normal with respect to $\delta$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-tangent-normal}
T_{\mathrm E}=\dot r\,\partial_{r}+\dot z\,\partial_{z},\qquad
N_{\mathrm E}=-\dot z\,\partial_{r}+\dot r\,\partial_{z}.
\end{equation}
We define the signed Euclidean curvature $\kappa_{\mathrm E}$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclidean-curvature-definition}
\frac{dT_{\mathrm E}}{d\lambda}=\kappa_{\mathrm E}N_{\mathrm E}.
\end{equation}
The metric induced on the meridional $(r,z)$--plane is $q=e^{\mu}\delta$ with $\delta=dr^{2}+dz^{2}$. If $\sigma$ denotes proper arclength along $\gamma$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proper-tangent-normal}
d\sigma=e^{\mu/2}d\lambda,\qquad
e_{s}=e^{-\mu/2}T_{\mathrm E},\qquad
n=e^{-\mu/2}N_{\mathrm E},
\end{equation}
where $e_s$ is the unit meridional tangent to $\Sigma$ and $n$ is its
spacelike unit normal. For the second fundamental form we use the convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extrinsic-curvature-convention}
K_{ab}=h_{a}{}^{\mu}h_{b}{}^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}.
\end{equation}
Reversing the orientation of $n$ reverses the sign of every component of
$K_{ab}$. The matching conditions are unaffected provided the normal
orientation is chosen consistently on the two sides.

\begin{lemma}[Fundamental forms of a symmetry-preserving timelike boundary]
\label{lem:symmetry-reduced-boundary-data}
Let $\Sigma$ be a smooth symmetry-preserving timelike hypersurface of
the form \eqref{eq:symmetry-generated-boundary}, contained in a regular
region with $r>0$. In the intrinsic basis $(\partial_t,\partial_\phi,e_s)$, with
$A,B\in\{t,\phi\}$, its first fundamental form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-fundamental-form-reduced}
h_{AB}=G_{AB},\qquad
h_{As}=0,\qquad
h_{ss}=1,
\end{equation}
and its second fundamental form is
\begin{align}
K_{AB}&=\frac{1}{2}\,n(G_{AB}),\label{eq:KAB-reduced}\\
K_{As}&=0,\label{eq:KAs-reduced}\\
K_{ss}&=e^{-\mu/2}\left[\frac{1}{2}N_{\mathrm E}(\mu)-\kappa_{\mathrm E}\right].
\label{eq:Kss-reduced}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restricting the block metric Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-block-metric} to $\Sigma$ gives
$g(\partial_A,\partial_B)=G_{AB}$ and $g(\partial_A,e_s)=0$. Because $e_s$ is normalized with respect to the physical meridional metric, $g(e_s,e_s)=1$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:first-fundamental-form-reduced}. The normal $n$ has only meridional components, and all metric coefficients are independent of $t$ and $\phi$. Hence, $[n,\partial_A]=0$. For vectors tangent to $\Sigma$, we have
$(\mathcal L_n g)(X,Y)=g(\nabla_Xn,Y)+g(X,\nabla_Yn)$, so the symmetry of the second fundamental form gives $2K_{AB}=(\mathcal L_n g)(\partial_A,\partial_B)=
n(G_{AB})$. This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:KAB-reduced}. Similarly,
$g(\partial_A,e_s)=0$ and the commutator $[n,e_s]$ is meridional. Block orthogonality therefore gives $(\mathcal L_n g)(\partial_A,e_s)=0$, and hence, $K_{As}=0$. It remains to determine $K_{ss}$. Let us set $\omega:=\mu/2$, so that the physical meridional metric is $q=e^{2\omega}\delta$. Its Levi--Civita connection is related to the Euclidean connection by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conformal-connection-formula}
\nabla^{q}_{X}Y=\nabla^{\mathrm E}_{X}Y+X(\omega)Y+Y(\omega)X-\delta(X,Y)\nabla^{\mathrm E}\omega.
\end{equation}
Since $e_s=e^{-\omega}T_{\mathrm E}$ and $n=e^{-\omega}N_{\mathrm E}$, we find
\begin{equation}
\nabla^{q}_{e_s}n=e^{-2\omega}\left[\nabla^{q}_{T_{\mathrm E}}N_{\mathrm E}
-T_{\mathrm E}(\omega)N_{\mathrm E}\right]=e^{-2\omega}\left[
\nabla^{\mathrm E}_{T_{\mathrm E}}N_{\mathrm E}+N_{\mathrm E}(\omega)T_{\mathrm E}\right].
\end{equation}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:euclidean-curvature-definition}, $\nabla^{\mathrm E}_{T_{\mathrm E}}N_{\mathrm E}=-\kappa_{\mathrm E}T_{\mathrm E}$. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^{q}_{e_s}n=e^{-2\omega}\bigl[N_{\mathrm E}(\omega)-\kappa_{\mathrm E}
\bigr]T_{\mathrm E}.
\end{equation}
Taking the scalar product with $e_s=e^{-\omega}T_{\mathrm E}$ gives
\begin{equation}
K_{ss}=q(\nabla^{q}_{e_s}n,e_s)=e^{-\omega}\bigl[
N_{\mathrm E}(\omega)-\kappa_{\mathrm E}\bigr].
\end{equation}
Using $\omega=\mu/2$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:Kss-reduced}.
\end{proof}

We now apply these formulas to a dust--vacuum matching. Let a superscript $-$ denote the rotating-dust interior and a superscript $+$ a stationary, axisymmetric, circular vacuum exterior. We assume a fixed symmetry-preserving identification of the two boundary hypersurfaces, so that the stationary and axial group orbits are identified and the period of the axial orbits is preserved~\cite{Vera2002CQG}. A common Killing basis is chosen under this identification. The induced tensors $h_{ab}^{\pm}$ and $K_{ab}^{\pm}$ may then be regarded as tensors on the same abstract boundary. We orient the unit
normals on both sides consistently from the dust region toward the vacuum region and define the jump of any boundary quantity $Q$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-definition}
[Q]:=Q^{+}-Q^{-}.
\end{equation}
Before normalising either boundary curve, let $u$ be one common intrinsic
parameter and write its two meridional embeddings as
$\gamma_\pm(u)=(r_\pm(u),z_\pm(u))$. The complete first-form comparison is
\begin{align}
G_{AB}^{+}|_{\Sigma}&=G_{AB}^{-}|_{\Sigma},
\label{eq:common-parameter-first-form-G}\\
e^{\mu_+}\bigl[(\dot r_+)^2+(\dot z_+)^2\bigr]
&=e^{\mu_-}\bigl[(\dot r_-)^2+(\dot z_-)^2\bigr].
\label{eq:common-parameter-first-form-meridional}
\end{align}
The mixed components vanish by block orthogonality. Only after
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:common-parameter-first-form-G}--\eqref{eq:common-parameter-first-form-meridional}
hold does their common induced metric provide one proper-arclength parameter
$\sigma$ and one abstract unit meridional tangent $e_s$. Normalising the two
curves separately would make $h_{ss}=1$ tautological and would not prove
that the boundary identification preserves physical arclength. With the
common normalisation now established, the formulas of
Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetry-reduced-boundary-data} can be compared component by
component.

\begin{proposition}[Local matching alternatives at a zero-density boundary]
\label{prop:matching-alternatives}
Let $(\mathcal M^{-},g^{-})$ be a $C^{2}$ stationary, axisymmetric, orthogonally transitive rotating-dust solution of the family considered
above, and let $\Sigma^{-}\subset\{\rho=0\}$ be a smooth timelike
symmetry-preserving hypersurface contained in a regular region with $r>0$. Let $(\mathcal M^{+},g^{+})$ be a $C^{2}$ stationary, axisymmetric, circular vacuum spacetime with a timelike symmetry-preserving boundary $\Sigma^{+}$. We fix a symmetry-preserving identification of $\Sigma^{-}$ with $\Sigma^{+}$ and use the common orientation specified above. Then exactly one of the following local
alternatives occurs.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I)]
{\bf{No standard distributional matching.}} If $[h_{ab}]\neq 0$, the induced metrics do not agree under the chosen identification. The resulting gluing is not a standard Darmois--Israel thin-shell matching. In particular, the discontinuity cannot be accounted for merely by assigning a finite surface stress tensor to $\Sigma$.

\item[(II)]
{\bf{Darmois matching without a thin shell.}} If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:darmois-invariant-conditions}
[h_{ab}]=0,\qquad
[K_{ab}]=0,
\end{equation}
the two spacetimes admit a Darmois matching across $\Sigma$. In the symmetry-adapted variables of Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetry-reduced-boundary-data}, these conditions are equivalent to
\begin{align}
[G_{AB}]&=0,\label{eq:darmois-G}\\
[n(G_{AB})]&=0,\label{eq:darmois-normal-G}\\
\left[e^{-\mu/2}\left(\frac{1}{2}N_{\mathrm E}(\mu)-\kappa_{\mathrm E}\right)
\right]&=0.\label{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature}
\end{align}
The mixed components impose no additional condition because $K_{As}^{\pm}=0$ on both sides.
\item[(III)]
{\bf{Matching with a nonzero Israel thin shell.}} If $[h_{ab}]=0$ and $[K_{ab}]\neq 0$, the gluing carries a nonzero surface stress-energy tensor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:israel-surface-tensor}
S_{ab}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G_{\rm N}}\left([K_{ab}]-h_{ab}[K]\right),\qquad
[K]:=h^{ab}[K_{ab}].
\end{equation}
Conversely, once the first fundamental form has been matched, $S_{ab}=0$ if and only if $[K_{ab}]=0$.
\end{enumerate}
The condition $\rho|_{\Sigma}=0$ does not distinguish alternatives (II) and (III). For a symmetry-preserving dust boundary, the dust four-velocity is tangent to $\Sigma$, so the normal matter projections entering the Gauss--Codazzi constraints vanish independently of the value of $\rho$. Imposing $\rho=0$ strengthens this statement to $T_{\mu\nu}|_{\Sigma}=0$, but it still does not impose Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature} on the vacuum exterior. More specifically,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)]
if $\Sigma$ lies inside an open vacuum region of an already constructed
$C^{2}$ solution, so that the same vacuum metric is defined on both
sides of the cut, restricting that metric to the two sides gives a
local Darmois matching;
\item[(b)]
if $\Sigma$ is an isolated regular component of $\mathcal Z_{+}$ or $\mathcal Z_{-}$, an isolated smooth component of $\mathcal C$, or a smooth intersection of $\mathcal C$ with one of the algebraic branches, then $\Sigma$ is only a candidate matching surface. It admits a shell-free vacuum exterior precisely when a vacuum solution exists whose boundary data satisfy
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature}. If the induced metric can be matched but these derivative conditions fail, the resulting continuous gluing carries the nonzero surface stress tensor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a non-null matching hypersurface, equality of the first fundamental forms is the preliminary condition required for a continuous metric under the chosen identification. If $[h_{ab}]\neq 0$, the standard Darmois--Israel thin-shell construction does not apply. This gives alternative (I). Suppose next that
$[h_{ab}]=0$. The common first fundamental form then defines the intrinsic metric $h_{ab}$ on $\Sigma$. A Darmois matching requires equality of the second
fundamental forms, $[K_{ab}]=0$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetry-reduced-boundary-data} on the two sides gives $[K_{AB}]=[n(G_{AB})]/2$, and $[K_{As}]=0$, together with
\begin{equation}
[K_{ss}]=\left[e^{-\mu/2}\left(\frac{1}{2}N_{\mathrm E}(\mu)-\kappa_{\mathrm E}
\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Together with $[h_{AB}]=[G_{AB}]=0$, these relations show that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:darmois-invariant-conditions} is equivalent to
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature}. This proves alternative (II). If instead $[h_{ab}]=0$ but $[K_{ab}]\neq 0$, the singular part of the Einstein tensor is described by the Israel surface tensor
Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor}, with the conventions for the normal and the jump fixed above. It remains only to verify that $S_{ab}=0$ cannot occur when $[K_{ab}]\neq 0$. Taking the trace of Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor} with the three-dimensional inverse metric $h^{ab}$ gives $h^{ab}S_{ab}=
[K]/(4\pi G_{\rm N})$. Hence, $S_{ab}=0$ implies $[K]=0$. Substitution back into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor} then gives $[K_{ab}]=0$. The converse is immediate. Thus, $S_{ab}=0$ if and only if $[K_{ab}]=0$, once $[h_{ab}]=0$, proving alternative (III). It remains to determine what additional information follows from the condition $\rho=0$. The dust four-velocity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dust-tangent-to-boundary}
u=(-H)^{-1/2}\bigl(\partial_t+\Omega\partial_\phi\bigr).
\end{equation}
Every symmetry-preserving hypersurface of the form Eq.~\eqref{eq:symmetry-generated-boundary} is tangent to both Killing directions. Therefore, $u$ is tangent to $\Sigma$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:no-normal-dust-flux}
u\cdot n=0,\qquad
T_{\mu\nu}n^{\nu}=\rho u_{\mu}(u\cdot n)=0.
\end{equation}
In particular, $T_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu=0$ and $T_{\mu\nu}n^\mu e_a^\nu=0$, irrespective of whether $\rho$ itself vanishes on $\Sigma$. For a Darmois matching, the Gauss and Codazzi equations require the corresponding Einstein-tensor projections to agree across the boundary, that is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-einstein-jumps}
[G_{\mu\nu}n^{\mu}n^{\nu}]=0,\qquad
[G_{\mu\nu}n^{\mu}e_{a}^{\nu}]=0.
\end{equation}
The tangency of the dust flow therefore satisfies these necessary normal constraints against a vacuum exterior even before imposing $\rho=0$. On a zero-density surface one has the stronger relation $T_{\mu\nu}|_{\Sigma}=0$. Neither fact, however, fixes the exterior values of $G_{AB}$, $n(G_{AB})$, or the meridional contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature}. Consequently, the scalar condition $\rho=0$ neither proves that a Darmois matching exists nor implies that a thin shell is unavoidable. Finally, if the cut is made inside an open vacuum region of an already constructed $C^{2}$ solution, the restrictions of the same metric to the two sides have identical first and second fundamental forms. This proves statement (a). If instead the zero-density component is isolated, no such vacuum collar is available. The alternatives above then show that a shell-free matching exists precisely when compatible vacuum boundary data satisfying Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature} can be realized. This proves statement (b).
\end{proof}

The preceding classification determines when a thin shell is present.
When the induced metric has been matched, the shell stress tensor can
also be written explicitly in terms of the symmetry-reduced jumps in
the second fundamental form.


\begin{corollary}[Symmetry-adapted form of the Israel surface tensor]
\label{cor:symmetry-adapted-shell-tensor}
Assume that the first fundamental forms have been matched, so that
$h_{AB}=G_{AB}$ defines a common Killing block on $\Sigma$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell-defect-data}
\Delta_{AB}:=[K_{AB}],\qquad
\Delta_{s}:=[K_{ss}],\qquad
\Delta_{G}:=G^{AB}\Delta_{AB},
\end{equation}
where $G^{AB}$ is the inverse of the common matrix $G_{AB}$. Then, the
Israel surface stress tensor has components
\begin{align}
8\pi G_{\rm N}\,S_{AB}&=-\Delta_{AB}+G_{AB}(\Delta_{G}+\Delta_{s}),\label{eq:shell-SAB}\\
S_{As}&=0,\label{eq:shell-SAs}\\
8\pi G_{\rm N}\,S_{ss}&=\Delta_{G}.\label{eq:shell-Sss}
\end{align}
Thus, the shell is completely determined by the four independent
components $(\Delta_{AB},\Delta_s)$ and vanishes exactly when all of
these components vanish.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because the first fundamental forms have been matched, $h_{AB}=G_{AB}$, $h_{ss}=1$, and $h_{As}=0$. Hence, $[K]=h^{ab}[K_{ab}]=G^{AB}\Delta_{AB}+\Delta_s=\Delta_G+\Delta_s$. Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor} gives Eqs.~\eqref{eq:shell-SAB}--\eqref{eq:shell-Sss}. The mixed component vanishes because $K_{As}^{+}=K_{As}^{-}=0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetry-reduced-boundary-data}. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:matching-alternatives} shows that, once the first
fundamental form has been matched, $S_{ab}=0$ if and only if $[K_{ab}]=0$. This is equivalent to $\Delta_{AB}=0$ and $\Delta_s=0$.
\end{proof}

Thus, a finite jump from positive dust density to vacuum does not by
itself constitute a thin shell. The shell content is controlled by the
jump in the second fundamental form.

The Darmois conditions also impose a useful invariant restriction on
the orbit-area radius. This is particularly important when
the dust interior is compared with a vacuum exterior written in
canonical Weyl coordinates.

\begin{corollary}[Matching of the orbit-area radius]
\label{cor:orbit-area-matching}
In the common stationary--axial Killing basis fixed above, let us define $\varpi:=\sqrt{-\det G}$. Every Darmois matching described in
Proposition~\ref{prop:matching-alternatives} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-area-matching-conditions}
[\varpi]=0,\qquad
[n(\varpi)]=0.
\end{equation}
For the rotating-dust interior,
$\varpi^{-}=r$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-metric-identities}. Hence, an
exterior written in canonical Weyl coordinates must satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl-radius-boundary-data}
\varpi^{+}|_{\Sigma}=r|_{\Sigma},\qquad
n(\varpi^{+})|_{\Sigma}=n(r)|_{\Sigma}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The condition $[G_{AB}]=0$ implies $[\det G]=0$, and therefore, $[\varpi]=0$. Since $\det G<0$ on the regular timelike boundary, differentiation of
$\varpi=\sqrt{-\det G}$ along the normal gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-determinant-identity}
2n(\log\varpi)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(G^{-1}nG\right).
\end{equation}
For a Darmois matching, both $G$ and $nG$ agree on the two sides by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G} and \eqref{eq:darmois-normal-G}. Hence, $[n(\log\varpi)]=0$. Together with $[\varpi]=0$, this gives $[n(\varpi)]=0$, which proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:orbit-area-matching-conditions}. Finally,
$\varpi^{-}=r$ for the rotating-dust interior, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:weyl-radius-boundary-data} follows immediately.
\end{proof}

For later comparison with a finite radial cutoff, it is useful to record
the corresponding specialization to a cylindrical boundary. Let us suppose
the dust boundary is the straight cylinder $r=R_{\Sigma}$, and write the vacuum exterior in canonical Weyl coordinates, using $\varpi$ as its canonical radial coordinate. By Corollary~\ref{cor:orbit-area-matching}, the corresponding exterior boundary is $\varpi=R_{\Sigma}$. Let us choose the normal to point toward increasing canonical radius, namely $r$ on the interior side and $\varpi$ on the exterior side. Since the generating curves are straight lines in their respective meridional coordinate planes, $\kappa_{\mathrm E}^{-}=\kappa_{\mathrm E}^{+}=0$. The Darmois conditions therefore reduce to
\begin{align}
G_{AB}^{+}&=G_{AB}^{-},\label{eq:cylindrical-G-match}\\
e^{\mu_+}\left(\frac{dz_+}{du}\right)^2
&=e^{\mu_-}\left(\frac{dz_-}{du}\right)^2,
\label{eq:cylindrical-meridional-first-form}\\
e^{-\mu_{+}/2}\partial_{\varpi}G_{AB}^{+}&=e^{-\mu_{-}/2}\partial_{r}G_{AB}^{-},
\label{eq:cylindrical-normal-G-match}\\
e^{-\mu_{+}/2}\partial_{\varpi}\mu_{+}&=e^{-\mu_{-}/2}\partial_{r}\mu_{-},
\label{eq:cylindrical-mu-match}
\end{align}
with all quantities evaluated on the boundary and $u$ one common intrinsic
parameter. Orient that parameter so that the two canonical longitudinal Weyl
coordinates are identified by $z_+(u)=z_-(u)=-u$. Then,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cylindrical-meridional-first-form} reduces to
$\mu_+=\mu_-$ on $\Sigma$. Indeed, for this oriented straight generating
curve the Euclidean curvature vanishes and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:euclidean-tangent-normal} gives
$N_{\mathrm E}^{-}=\partial_r$ and
$N_{\mathrm E}^{+}=\partial_{\varpi}$, consistently with the chosen direction
toward increasing canonical radius. Equations~\eqref{eq:KAB-reduced} and
\eqref{eq:Kss-reduced} then give
\begin{equation}
K_{AB}^{-}=\frac12e^{-\mu_{-}/2}\partial_rG_{AB}^{-},\qquad
K_{AB}^{+}=\frac12e^{-\mu_{+}/2}\partial_{\varpi}G_{AB}^{+},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
K_{ss}^{-}=\frac12e^{-\mu_{-}/2}\partial_r\mu_{-},\qquad
K_{ss}^{+}=\frac12e^{-\mu_{+}/2}\partial_{\varpi}\mu_{+}.
\end{equation}
Equating the full induced metric and these components of the second
fundamental form yields Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cylindrical-G-match}--
\eqref{eq:cylindrical-mu-match}. If the induced metric has been matched
but any of the derivative conditions fails, the resulting continuous
gluing carries the nonzero Israel surface tensor Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor}.

\subsection{Finite cutoffs and global vacuum compatibility}
\label{subsec:global-vacuum-compatibility}

For the edge calibration,
$R_{\rho}^{\mathrm{exact}}\in\mathcal Z_{+}$ is an exact
density-positivity cutoff, not a constructed material boundary.
Proposition~\ref{prop:constant-l-obstruction} excludes the spatially
constant ansatz on an open annulus, whereas
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-flat-trace-existence} provides only local
nonconstant realizations. A shell-free completion would additionally
require a regular three-dimensional boundary carrying the full data
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-matching-data-summary}
\left(G_{AB},\,n(G_{AB}),\,K_{ss}\right)\Big|_{\Sigma}
\end{equation}
and a compatible vacuum exterior. If a particular asymptotically flat stationary vacuum exterior is specified, its induced metric must first satisfy
Eq.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G} under the chosen boundary identification. If
the induced metric agrees but one or more of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:darmois-normal-G} and \eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature} fail, the matching carries the
nonzero surface stress tensor Eq.~\eqref{eq:israel-surface-tensor}. If
Eq.~\eqref{eq:darmois-G} itself fails, the proposed identification does
not define a standard Israel thin-shell matching. For the global statements in this paper, we use the following standard isolated-body class.

\begin{definition}[Admissible isolated vacuum completion]
\label{def:admissible-vacuum-completion}
An admissible isolated vacuum completion of the dust interior across
$\Sigma$ is a connected exterior spacetime $(\mathcal M^{+},g^{+})$
satisfying all of the following conditions
\begin{enumerate}
\item
$g^{+}$ is $C^{2}$ up to $\Sigma$ and smooth in the exterior,
$R_{\mu\nu}[g^{+}]=0$, and the exterior is stationary, axisymmetric,
and circular, with commuting Killing fields $k^{+}$ and $m^{+}$ whose
axial orbits have period $2\pi$;
\item
$\Sigma^{+}$ is a smooth connected timelike
symmetry-preserving boundary, the identification with the dust boundary
preserves the Killing basis and axial period, and the Darmois conditions
\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature} hold, so that no surface layer is present;
\item
the exterior domain contains no additional boundary component,
horizon, curvature singularity, or conical defect, and every axis
segment in its closure is regular;
\item
the orbit space $Q^+$ of the $\mathbb R\times U(1)$ action is allowed to
be multiply connected. With $\varpi=\sqrt{-\det G^{+}}$, every period of
$*_{q}d\varpi$ and of the descended twist one-form vanishes. The resulting
potentials are single valued, $d\varpi\neq0$ in the canonical-coordinate
region, and $(\varpi,\zeta)$ is injective and proper wherever global Weyl
coordinates are invoked;
\item
the exterior has one asymptotically flat end satisfying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:af-cartesian-falloff}, with $k^{+}$ normalized to unit
time translation and the angular coordinate asymptotically nonrotating.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The local matching conditions must be distinguished from existence in
this global class. Proposition~\ref{prop:matching-alternatives} gives
exact conditions at $\Sigma$, but satisfying them does not construct an exterior obeying Definition~\ref{def:admissible-vacuum-completion}. In the Ernst formulation, the matched metric and its normal derivatives encode both Dirichlet- and Neumann-type boundary information. These data cannot be prescribed independently. Even for an exterior Dirichlet problem, arbitrary boundary values need not be admissible \cite{AnsorgEtAl2002PRD}. The global results cited here have different scopes. Mars and Senovilla prove uniqueness of the asymptotically flat stationary, axisymmetric vacuum exterior generated by a given finite rotating body within their
standard isolated-body and harmonic-map setting, once the matching data and the remaining additive normalization of the twist potential are fixed \cite{MarsSenovilla1998MPLA}. This is a uniqueness theorem
conditional on existence, not a general nonlinear existence theorem.
Hauser and Ernst give a constructive procedure for a given stationary, axisymmetric perfect-fluid interior, but the resulting vacuum exterior is not assumed to be asymptotically flat and the method does not by itself establish all of the regularity and global conditions in Definition~\ref{def:admissible-vacuum-completion}
\cite{HauserErnst2001GRG}. MacCallum, Mars, and Vera derive necessary
and sufficient compatibility conditions for the overdetermined boundary data only in a perturbative problem through second order about a static isolated body \cite{MacCallumMarsVera2007PRD}. Accordingly, none of these references supplies a general existence theorem for the zero-density branches considered here.

An isolated regular zero-density candidate surface must therefore pass two logically distinct tests
\begin{enumerate}
\item
the local Darmois conditions
\eqref{eq:darmois-G}--\eqref{eq:darmois-meridional-curvature};
\item
existence of a vacuum solution satisfying every global condition
in Definition~\ref{def:admissible-vacuum-completion} and realizing the matched boundary data.
\end{enumerate}
Failure of the first test has the consequences classified in
Proposition~\ref{prop:matching-alternatives}. Passing the local test
does not guarantee the global one. If no admissible isolated vacuum
completion realizes the matched data, then no exterior exists in the
class of Definition~\ref{def:admissible-vacuum-completion}. Introducing
a thin shell changes the boundary data but does not ensure global
existence.



\section{Observable scope: ZAMO speed and spectroscopic frequency shifts}
\label{sec:observable-scope}

Reference~\cite{Astesiano2022EPJC} explicitly notes that the ZAMO speed
$v=\eta/r$ need not coincide with the velocity inferred observationally
for an external galaxy. The subsequent observational proposal
\cite{Astesiano2022Observational} therefore considered photon frequency
shifts. Direct stationary-axisymmetric redshift formulae and their inverse
reconstruction limitations were developed in
Ref.~\cite{HerreraAguilarEtAl2013MNRAS}, while a recent locally inertial and
radially locked construction provides a complementary observer prescription
\cite{FontanaCacciatoriPeron2026arXiv}. Recent work has also calculated gravitational-lensing observables
and image asymmetries directly in stationary, axisymmetric rotating-dust
geometries \cite{RuggieroAstesiano2025PRD}. Here we examine this distinction using only the exact
consequences of stationarity, axisymmetry, and geometric optics, without
introducing an additional weak-field ray model. We first derive the invariant frequency ratio between a circular emitter and a circular observer in terms of the Killing constants of the connecting null geodesic. We then show that the scalar field $v$ alone does not determine this observable, because the measured shift also depends on the emitter and observer frames, the metric functions, and
the null-geodesic boundary-value problem. Finally, we explain why a
spectroscopic prediction for an isolated system necessarily depends on
the global completion of the dust region.

\subsection{Invariant emitter--observer frequency shift}

Let $k$ and $m$ denote the stationary and axial Killing fields, respectively, with their normalization and the period of the axial orbits fixed as above. Let $\gamma$ be a future-directed, affinely parametrized null geodesic connecting an emission event $p_{\rm e}$ to an observation event $p_{\rm o}$, and let $\dot\gamma$ denote its tangent vector. For any future-directed unit timelike observer $u$, the measured photon frequency is the invariant scalar
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant-observer-frequency}
\omega(u,\dot\gamma):=-g(u,\dot\gamma)>0.
\end{equation}
We consider circular emitter and observer worldlines whose four-velocities
have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-source-observer}
u_a=U_a\bigl(k+\Omega_a m\bigr),\qquad
a\in\{{\rm e},{\rm o}\},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-normalization}
U_a=\left[-g\bigl(k+\Omega_a m,k+\Omega_a m\bigr)\right]^{-1/2}=\left[
-\left(g_{tt}+2\Omega_a g_{t\phi}+\Omega_a^2g_{\phi\phi}\right)_a\right]^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
The normalization is well defined precisely where the circular direction
$k+\Omega_a m$ is timelike.

\begin{proposition}[Invariant emitter--observer frequency ratio]
\label{prop:invariant-redshift-formula}
Along $\gamma$, let us define the Killing constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:photon-killing-integrals}
E:=-g(\dot\gamma,k),\qquad
L:=g(\dot\gamma,m).
\end{equation}
Then, $E$ and $L$ are constant along the null geodesic. If $E\neq 0$, we define the conserved impact parameter $b:=L/E$. For the circular emitter and observer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:circular-source-observer}, let $\omega_a:=\omega(u_a,\dot\gamma)$ with $a\in\{{\rm e},{\rm o}\}$. Their exact frequency ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-killing-redshift}
1+z:=\frac{\omega_{\rm e}}{\omega_{\rm o}}=\frac{U_{\rm e}}{U_{\rm o}}
\frac{1-\Omega_{\rm e}b}{1-\Omega_{\rm o}b}.
\end{equation}
For a dust emitter, Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-velocity-relations} gives
$U_{\rm e}=(-H_{\rm e})^{-1/2}$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dust-redshift-general-observer}
1+z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-H_{\rm e}}\,U_{\rm o}}\frac{1-\Omega_{\rm e}b}{1-\Omega_{\rm o}b}.
\end{equation}
If an asymptotically flat completion exists and the observer approaches
physical rest in the asymptotically nonrotating frame, assume explicitly
that
\begin{equation}
g(k,k)_{\rm o}\to-1,\qquad
\Omega_{\rm o}^{2}g_{\phi\phi}|_{\rm o}\to0,\qquad
\Omega_{\rm o}g_{t\phi}|_{\rm o}\to0.
\label{eq:asymptotic-observer-rest}
\end{equation}
For a family of rays, also assume $\Omega_{\rm o}b\to0$. Then
$U_{\rm o}\to1$ and the exact ratio has the limiting form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dust-redshift-asymptotic-observer}
1+z\longrightarrow\frac{1-\Omega_{\rm e}b}{\sqrt{-H_{\rm e}}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $\dot\gamma$ is tangent to an affinely parametrized null geodesic
and $k$ is a Killing field, we find
\begin{equation}\label{eq:killing-energy-conservation-proof}
\dot\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}g(\dot\gamma,k)=\dot\gamma^{\mu}
\nabla_{\mu}\!\left(\dot\gamma^{\nu}k_{\nu}\right)=
k_{\nu}\dot\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\dot\gamma^{\nu}+\dot\gamma^{\mu}\dot\gamma^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}k_{\nu}=0.
\end{equation}
The first term vanishes by the geodesic equation. The second vanishes
because $\dot\gamma^{\mu}\dot\gamma^{\nu}$ is symmetric, whereas the
Killing equation gives $\nabla_{(\mu}k_{\nu)}=0$. Hence, $E=-g(\dot\gamma,k)$ is constant along $\gamma$. The same argument applied to the axial Killing field $m$ shows that $L=g(\dot\gamma,m)$ is constant. At either endpoint,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:invariant-observer-frequency} and \eqref{eq:circular-source-observer} give
\begin{equation}
\omega_a=-g(\dot\gamma,u_a)=-U_a\left[g(\dot\gamma,k)+\Omega_a g(\dot\gamma,m)
\right]=U_a(E-\Omega_aL).
\end{equation}
For $E\neq0$ and $b=L/E$, this becomes $\omega_a=E\,U_a(1-\Omega_a b)$. Taking the ratio of the emitted and observed frequencies yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-killing-redshift}. For a dust emitter, Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-velocity-relations} gives $U_{\rm e}=(-H_{\rm e})^{-1/2}$, which yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:dust-redshift-general-observer}. Finally,
$$
U_{\rm o}^{-2}
=-g_{tt}-2\Omega_{\rm o}g_{t\phi}
-\Omega_{\rm o}^{2}g_{\phi\phi}.
$$
The three conditions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-observer-rest} give
$U_{\rm o}\to1$, and $\Omega_{\rm o}b\to0$ controls the remaining ray
factor. This proves the limiting relation
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dust-redshift-asymptotic-observer}.
\end{proof}

A spectroscopic rotation curve requires a family of such rays.
Accordingly, the impact parameter $b=L/E$ is fixed by the global
source--observer boundary-value problem rather than by the local dust
state.

\subsection{Non-identifiability and global completion}

The insufficiency of $v$ as observational input is already visible in a local orthonormal frame. Let $u_{\rm Z}$ denote the ZAMO four-velocity at the emission event, and let $e_{\hat\phi}$ be the unit azimuthal spatial vector in the ZAMO frame. The dust four-velocity can then be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dust-zamo-decomposition}
u_{\rm e}=\Gamma\bigl(u_{\rm Z}+v e_{\hat\phi}\bigr),\qquad
\Gamma=(1-v^{2})^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
At the same event, we can decompose the future-directed null tangent as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:photon-zamo-decomposition}
\dot\gamma=\omega_{\rm Z}\bigl(u_{\rm Z}+\widehat\nu\bigr),\qquad
g(\widehat\nu,\widehat\nu)=1,\qquad
g(u_{\rm Z},\widehat\nu)=0,
\end{equation}
where
$\omega_{\rm Z}:=-g(u_{\rm Z},\dot\gamma)>0$ is the photon frequency measured by the ZAMO. Let us define the azimuthal direction cosine by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:azimuthal-direction-cosine}
\cos\alpha:=g(\widehat\nu,e_{\hat\phi})\in[-1,1].
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-doppler-direction-factor}
\omega_{\rm e}=-g(u_{\rm e},\dot\gamma)=\Gamma\,\omega_{\rm Z}
\bigl(1-v\cos\alpha\bigr).
\end{equation}
Thus, even the local Doppler factor depends on the photon direction, not
only on the magnitude of the ZAMO speed $v$.

\begin{proposition}[Spectroscopic non-identifiability from the ZAMO speed]
\label{prop:zamo-nonidentifiability}
For the class of stationary, axisymmetric observations described above,
the scalar field $v=\eta/r$ does not by itself determine the observed
frequency shift. In particular, there is no functional $\mathcal Z$ of
$v$ alone, independent of the observer and of the connecting null ray, such that $z_{\rm obs}=\mathcal Z[v]$ for every admissible emitter, observer, and connecting null geodesic. More specifically, knowledge of $v$ alone does not in general determine the additional data $H_{\rm e}$, $\Omega_{\rm e}$, $u_{\rm o}$, and $b=L/E$, or the map from a detector-screen position to the corresponding emission event. Consequently, exact or approximate flatness of $v(r,0)$ does not by itself imply exact or approximate flatness of a spectroscopically inferred rotation curve. The converse implication does not follow either.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:local-doppler-direction-factor} already shows that the local emission data are not determined by $v$ alone. For a fixed rest-frame spectral-line frequency $\omega_{\rm e}$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zamo-photon-energy-fixed-line}
\omega_{\rm Z}=\frac{\omega_{\rm e}}{\Gamma(1-v\cos\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
Thus, even with the emitter and its ZAMO speed fixed, the photon energy
measured in the ZAMO frame depends on the emission direction
$\alpha$. Observer dependence provides an independent obstruction. Let us fix the spacetime, the emission event, the connecting null geodesic, and the
observation event. At the observation event, future-directed unit timelike circular observers form one branch of the unit hyperbola in the Lorentzian Killing plane spanned by $k$ and $m$. Choose an orthonormal basis $(e_{\hat 0},e_{\hat\phi})$ of that plane. Such observers may be parametrized locally as $u(\beta)=\cosh\beta\,e_{\hat 0}+\sinh\beta\,e_{\hat\phi}$. Their measured photon frequency is $-g(\dot\gamma,u(\beta))=A\cosh\beta+B\sinh\beta$ for constants $A$ and $B$ determined by $\dot\gamma$. This expression
could be constant on an interval of $\beta$ only if $A=B=0$. In that
case, $\dot\gamma$ would be orthogonal to the entire Killing plane. Because the metric is block diagonal between the Killing and meridional
planes, $\dot\gamma$ would then lie entirely in the positive-definite
meridional plane. A nonzero vector in that plane cannot be null, giving
a contradiction. Hence, two admissible circular observers at the same observation event can measure different photon frequencies while the spacetime, emitter, ray, and scalar field $v$ are unchanged. No observer-independent
functional of $v$ alone can therefore reproduce every admissible frequency ratio. Even after the observer and viewing configuration have been fixed,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-killing-redshift} still contains the ray constant
$b=L/E$. Its value is selected by the null-geodesic boundary-value
problem and depends on the metric and on the endpoint conditions. In
the rotating-dust parametrization, specifying $v$ determines
$\eta=rv$, but it does not by itself determine the additional function
$H$ or the corresponding angular velocity $\Omega$. The complete metric
is also required to determine the connecting null ray and the
screen-to-source map. Thus, the additional data $H_{\rm e}$, $\Omega_{\rm e}$, $u_{\rm o}$, and $b=L/E$ are not contained in the scalar speed profile alone, which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a statement about insufficiency of observational
input, not a solution-space degeneracy theorem. It does not assert the
existence of two globally regular solutions of the full Einstein--dust
system having the same field $v$ and the same complete observational
setup but producing different redshifts. Establishing such a stronger
statement would require a separate existence and nonuniqueness analysis
for the full boundary-value problem. What has been proved is the
logically prior fact that $v$ alone does not specify the observer
four-velocity, the photon four-momentum, or the global ray connecting
the emitter to the detector. It therefore cannot determine the
spectroscopic frequency shift by itself.

This non-identifiability becomes especially important when the dust
region is embedded in an isolated spacetime. The asymptotic and matching
results of Sects.~\ref{sec:asymptotic-obstruction} and
\ref{sec:vacuum-matching} show that the missing propagation data cannot
be supplied by the constant-$\ell$ interior alone.

\begin{corollary}[Global-completion alternatives for the observable]
\label{cor:observable-global-completion-dichotomy}
Consider a nonzero constant-$\ell$ dust branch and a spectroscopic observation intended to be made by an observer at rest in an asymptotically nonrotating isolated end. Then, the following alternatives have distinct observational consequences.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
If the retained-order logarithmic branch of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:logarithmic-omega}--
\eqref{eq:logarithmic-metric-components} is promoted to the actual behaviour on the unbounded end, then Proposition~\ref{prop:asymptotic-obstruction} excludes asymptotic flatness. The asymptotic specialization $U_{\rm o}\to1$ and $\Omega_{\rm o}\to0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dust-redshift-asymptotic-observer} is therefore not available within that branch.
\item
If the dust region terminates at a finite symmetry-preserving hypersurface and is Darmois matched to a stationary, axisymmetric vacuum exterior, then Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-killing-redshift} must be evaluated in the globally matched spacetime. The exterior geometry, together with the endpoint conditions, enters the null-geodesic boundary-value problem and therefore affects both the conserved ratio $b=L/E$ selected for the observed ray and the screen-to-source map. The interior data $(v,\ell)$ alone do not determine the observable.
\item
If the solution instead leaves the constant-$\ell$ branch smoothly before reaching the asymptotic end, then the transition region and the eventual exterior likewise form indispensable parts of the forward model. The constant-$\ell$ interior alone again does not determine the observed frequency shift.
\end{enumerate}
If a thin shell is present at a matching surface, its surface data add
to the information required by the global propagation problem rather
than eliminating that dependence.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first alternative follows directly from Proposition~\ref{prop:asymptotic-obstruction}. If the retained-order logarithmic branch is extended as the actual asymptotic metric for $\ellzero\neq 0$, the required asymptotically flat end does not exist. The asymptotic observer limit used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dust-redshift-asymptotic-observer} therefore cannot be taken within that branch. For the second alternative, a symmetry-preserving Darmois matching identifies the stationary and axial Killing fields across the boundary. The quantities $E$ and $L$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:photon-killing-integrals} are consequently the conserved
Killing quantities of the global null ray. Their ratio $b=L/E$ is selected by the complete boundary-value problem connecting the emitter to the observer. Proposition~\ref{prop:matching-alternatives} shows that the required vacuum exterior is not determined merely by the condition $\rho=0$. Hence, neither the exterior geometry nor the resulting spectroscopic observable is fixed by the interior data $(v,\ell)$ alone. In the third alternative, the constant-$\ell$ description ceases to apply before the asymptotic region is reached. The transition geometry and the subsequent exterior then enter the same global null-geodesic problem and must therefore be included in the forward model. This proves all three alternatives.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The spectroscopic forward problem}

A complete observational prediction requires a forward model that maps
the spacetime geometry, the emitter and observer worldlines, the source
geometry, and the observational prescription to a frequency-shift map
on the detector screen. This procedure can be represented schematically as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-forward-map}
\bigl(g,\,u_{\rm e},\,u_{\rm o},\,\mathcal S,\,\iota,\,\mathcal P\bigr)
\longmapsto\{\gamma_{\Theta}\}
\longmapsto z_{\rm obs}(\Theta),
\end{equation}
where $\Theta$ labels a detector-screen position, $\gamma_{\Theta}$ is the null geodesic selected by the corresponding source--observer boundary-value problem, $\mathcal S$ denotes the source and emission geometry, $\iota$ is the disk inclination, and $\mathcal P$ specifies the treatment of systemic, peculiar, and
cosmological frequency shifts together with the line-profile inference
procedure. If the dust region has a finite boundary, the geometry entering this
forward problem must include the matched exterior and any transition region or surface layer that is present. Likewise, replacing the exact null propagation by an approximate ray model requires quantitative control of the resulting error in the screen-to-source map over the radial domain of interest. To transfer the approximate-flatness bounds of Sect.~\ref{sec:approx-flat-obstruction} to a spectroscopically inferred velocity profile, one would need an additional comparison between the observable profile and the local ZAMO speed. For example, a sufficient route would be to establish, on the relevant interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:required-observable-slope-control}
\left|\frac{d\log v_{\rm spec}}{d\log R_{\rm obs}}-\frac{d\log v(r,0)}{d\log r}
\right|\leqslant\delta
\end{equation}
for some prescribed tolerance $\delta\geqslant 0$ measuring the allowed
difference between the two logarithmic slopes. Here, $R_{\rm obs}$
denotes the projected observational radius and $r$ the emission radius. Such an estimate would have to be supplemented by controlled monotone bounds relating $R_{\rm obs}$ to $r$. Both velocity profiles must also remain positive on the interval so that the logarithmic slopes are defined. No comparison of this kind follows from the velocity-field equation alone. It requires the completed spacetime, the emitter and observer frames, and the null-geodesic propagation problem developed above. Constructing and testing this full spectroscopic forward model is therefore a natural subject for subsequent numerical work.

\section{Conclusions and outlook}
\label{sec:conclusions_outlook}

The outcome is a branch-specific phase diagram rather than a blanket
no-go statement for rotating dust. The broad finite-radius and matching
tensions were already visible in earlier work; the advance here is to make
the constant-$\ell$ mechanisms sharp, to identify their singular strata,
and to connect local realizability to the genuinely global tests that a
galaxy model must pass.

Locally, the proposed nonzero constant field fails both the exact and
retained-order VFEs on every open radial region. Nonconstant fields can
nevertheless realize an exactly flat equatorial trace away from the axis.
The auxiliary map explains both facts: its regular sheet linearizes the VFE,
whereas its lower density boundary is a Whitney fold at which the Legendre
map and PDE symbol degenerate. Classical regular sheets are automatically
analytic, but their elliptic Cauchy continuation is Hadamard unstable.
Runge approximation enlarges a local germ only on a protected compact core;
it does not manufacture a global physical solution.

The sharp local freedom is therefore compatible with strong global
rigidity. A regular metric axis forces $H'(0)=0$, excluding every nonzero
constant-$\ell$ sheet that reaches it. Under complete-half-plane and decay
hypotheses, the remaining $\ellzero=0$ branch is Minkowski vacuum. On
axis-avoiding annuli, density and the invariant vertical-maximum condition
give finite span bounds. The nonpositive-$\ellzero$ bound and the
positive-$\ellzero$ exact-flat limit are sharp through genuine analytic PDE
solutions; for positive tolerance and positive $\ellzero$, the new strict
gap proves that the earlier comparison estimate was not optimal and isolates
a precise state-constrained control problem that remains open.

At infinity, the retained-order logarithmic branch is not a standard
asymptotically flat end. An exact uniformly subluminal nonzero
constant-$\ellzero$ tail instead locks $rv$ to one constant. Nonnegative
density then forces an eventual vacuum plateau whose local geometry is a
flat helical quotient. Nonzero affine time holonomy is possible only on an
axis-excised domain; it cannot extend within the same flat region to a
regular axial fixed point. A compact axis-avoiding body is naturally a solid
torus, so its vacuum orbit surface carries period obstructions that a simply
connected exterior definition would erase by assumption.

At a finite boundary, $\rho=0$ supplies neither an exterior nor a shell-free
matching. The complete first and second fundamental forms, equivalently the
GHY boundary momentum together with the induced metric, must agree. Even
then the nonlinear vacuum exterior must be constructed; the Calder\'on graph
derived here is only its gauge-fixed linearized compatibility test. The
weighted scalar action, its Noether currents, Poisson cocycle, and
multisymplectic form organize the auxiliary equation, but they are not the
Einstein--dust phase space. The latter requires the vortical Brown action,
gravitational constraints, physical boundary terms, and covariant charges.
The neutral EMDA rigidity and duality analysis is conditional on a different
field theory and supplies no hidden completion of the dust model.

Finally, $v$ is a local ZAMO speed, not a spectroscopic rotation curve. A
prediction requires the completed metric, emitter and observer frames, and
the connecting null-geodesic boundary-value problem. Thus the constant-field
edge construction does not by itself define a globally viable isolated
galaxy. The results do not exclude nonconstant-$\ell$ interiors, separately
constructed finite exteriors, pressure-supported or kinetic matter,
nonstandard asymptotics, or an observer-complete model that survives all of
the tests above.

\subsection{Natural continuation: from admissibility to physical prediction}

The present analysis supplies admissibility conditions rather than a
completed galaxy model. A natural continuation is therefore not to
begin by fitting another prescribed ZAMO-speed profile, but to construct
a complete spacetime, establish its dynamical viability, and only then
compare its predicted observables with data. Such a program has several
distinct components.

\begin{enumerate}

\item
\emph{Close the remaining sharp control problem.} Determine the exact exit-time
supremum for the positive-$\ellzero$, positive-$\varepsilon$ system in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:positive-l-control-system}, including boundary arcs and density
tangencies, and prove that any extremal trace lifts to an admissible local
PDE solution. This is the one unresolved sharpness question in the present
annular theory.
\item
\emph{Construct globally admissible backgrounds.} A finite parametrization of $H$ or $\ell$, together with an appropriate matter closure, should be chosen so that a finite-thickness galaxy can be constructed subject to the field equations rather than imposed kinematically. Pressureless dust remains an important control case, but anisotropic-fluid or Einstein--Vlasov descriptions provide natural extensions when velocity dispersion, multistreaming, or pressure
support cannot be neglected. Regular-axis and equatorial conditions, density positivity, causality, and either a finite free boundary or a controlled transition to the outer region must be imposed as part of the same construction.
\item
\emph{Solve the interior and exterior as a coupled boundary-value
problem.} For a finite matter configuration, the interior fields, the location of the matter boundary, and the stationary vacuum exterior should be determined consistently rather than obtained by attaching an exterior after fitting the interior. The symmetry-reduced Darmois data derived in this work provide the local interface conditions. A surface layer should be introduced only when it is part of the physical model, not as an implicit replacement for incompatible boundary data. Numerical continuation from analytically controlled limits, using spectral or high-order finite-element methods, is one possible approach to the resulting nonlinear free-boundary problem.
\item
\emph{Establish numerical reliability and global regularity.} Candidate solutions should be tested by convergence under domain and resolution refinement, residuals of all independent Einstein and matter equations, and explicit checks of the axis and junction conditions. Sensitivity to boundary placement and parametrization should also be quantified. Curvature invariants, energy conditions, causal pathologies, and the appropriate global charges provide further independent diagnostics.
\item \emph{Determine dynamical stability.} The existence of circular motion in a stationary geometry is not sufficient for an astrophysical galaxy. Radial and vertical epicyclic stability provide useful first diagnostics where those notions are applicable, but the matter and geometry ultimately require a coupled
perturbation analysis. For dust, caustic formation and multistreaming deserve particular attention. Fluid and kinetic extensions introduce their own pressure, dispersion, and phase-space modes. Selected stationary configurations should eventually be tested by nonlinear evolution.
\item
\emph{Compute observables from the complete spacetime.} Implement the forward map formulated in Sect.~\ref{sec:observable-scope}, including ray tracing through the interior, transition region, and exterior. In particular, quantify the relation between the inferred spectroscopic speed and the local ZAMO speed before applying the approximate-flatness bounds derived here.
\item \emph{Compare viable relativistic models with conventional mass
models.} Only after the preceding consistency tests have been passed is a
statistical comparison with galaxy data meaningful. Rotation, vertical-dynamics, photometric, gas, and lensing measurements should be treated with common baryonic nuisance parameters and controlled systematics. Approximate flatness should be defined quantitatively, for example through a logarithmic-slope interval, rather than by visual inspection. Parameter identifiability and model complexity should be included when comparing the relativistic construction with standard baryons-plus-halo baselines.
\end{enumerate}

This program is deliberately falsifiable. It may show that no regular,
positive-density, stable, globally completed solution produces an
observable effect of the required magnitude. Such a result would extend
the present mathematical obstructions into a stronger physical
exclusion. Conversely, if viable configurations exist, they must make
joint predictions for spectroscopic shifts, vertical dynamics, lensing,
and other observables that distinguish them from conventional mass
models. The present work supplies the consistency conditions that such
future constructions must satisfy before either outcome can be assessed.

\section{Symbolic reproducibility checks}
\label{app:symbolic-reproducibility}

The accompanying package separates exact algebraic certificates from
analytical proofs. From the revised manuscript directory, the complete
symbolic suite is run with
\begin{verbatim}
make verify
\end{verbatim}
and the vector figures are regenerated with
\begin{verbatim}
make figures
\end{verbatim}
The suite contains the following independent layers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{constant\_ell\_checks.mpl} verifies the original eleven exact
reductions: the exact-minus-retained identity, both constant-field
residuals, the Killing-block determinant, density factorization and roots,
the point-linearization identity, the logarithmic trace identity, the fold
factorization, and the optimizer equation.

\item \texttt{verify\_variational.mpl} and
\texttt{verify\_variational\_sympy.py} independently verify the pulled-back
Euler--Lagrange equation, fibre Hessian, De Donder--Weyl Hamiltonian,
Poincar\'e--Cartan pullback, multisymplectic and Noether identities,
Pohozaev formula, exact modes, distributional reflected-layer coefficient,
and the reduced Ernst equations.

\item \texttt{rifsimp\_branches.mpl} records every denominator and treats
$r=0$, $\eta=0$, $v=0$, $A=0$, and $\ellzero=0$ separately before applying
\texttt{rifsimp}. It recovers the nonlinear VFE on the regular sheet,
proves that no two-dimensional physical solution lies wholly in the fold,
and verifies the exact Frobenius identity for the $\mu$ quadrature.

\item \texttt{thomas\_certificates.mpl} uses
\texttt{DifferentialThomas} to obtain a disjoint four-component
differential decomposition. It retains the two polynomial closures at
$\eta=0$ instead of silently dividing them away, and independently proves
the first-prolongation constant-field incompatibility and the symbol-rank
jump.

\item \texttt{verify\_approx\_flat\_sharpness.py} checks the exact control
drift, all comparison differences used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:refined-positive-l-span}, and a seeded set of $5000$
admissible state comparisons. The randomized comparisons test the encoded
inequalities; the proof of the theorem remains analytical.

\item \texttt{audit\_core\_symbolic.py} and
\texttt{audit\_physical\_string\_sympy.py} independently rederive selected
density, trace, asymptotic, matching, covariant-action, frame, duality, and
axidilaton identities without importing the primary verification routines.

\item \texttt{generate\_figures.py} regenerates the three vector figures and
the machine-readable density-boundary CSV. It checks the plotted roots and
fold identity before writing any artifact; the curves labelled as
representative radial paths remain explicitly schematic.

\item \texttt{check\_manuscript.py} audits citation keys, labels, metadata,
house-style inequalities and delimiters, and release-blocking build-log
diagnostics. The atomic \texttt{revision\_tracker.py} and the generated
\texttt{REVISION\_STATUS.md} provide a restart-safe claim--evidence record.
\end{enumerate}

Every scientific certificate terminates with an error when an asserted
identity or branch count fails. Maple 2022 uses a local kernel socket on some
systems, but the scripts make no network request. These calculations certify
the encoded local algebra and differential ideals. They do not certify axis
regularity, global existence, positivity away from the tested hypotheses,
nonlinear vacuum matching, stability, or observational viability.
